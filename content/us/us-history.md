# US history fast pass: Revolution to today

## What this is and why it matters

American history is a 400-year arc that turned 13 scattered Atlantic colonies into the wealthiest, most militarized, most culturally dominant country on the planet. The interesting part is that almost every contemporary debate in the US, race, guns, federal power, immigration, religion, the role of the courts, has roots somewhere in this sweep. If you know the story end to end, the news stops feeling random. It starts feeling like the next chapter of a book you already read.

## Colonization (1607-1763)

- **Jamestown, Virginia (1607)** was the first permanent English settlement. Tobacco saved it. By 1619 the colony was importing enslaved Africans, the same year it created its first elected assembly. Both founding facts in the same year.
- The **Pilgrims** landed at Plymouth in 1620 on the Mayflower. Religious dissenters, not commercial settlers. They wrote the Mayflower Compact on board, a tiny self-governance document that gets cited as a seed of American democracy.
- By the mid-1700s there were 13 British colonies along the Atlantic, mostly English speaking, mostly Protestant, deeply commercial in the north, plantation and slave-based in the south. They thought of themselves as British.

## Revolution (1763-1783)

- After Britain won the French and Indian War in 1763, it tried to tax the colonies to pay for it. Stamp Act, Sugar Act, Tea Act. The colonists, who had no representation in Parliament, refused.
- The **Boston Tea Party (1773)** was a protest where colonists dumped 342 chests of British tea into Boston Harbor. Britain responded with punitive laws. Fighting broke out at Lexington and Concord in 1775.
- **Thomas Jefferson** drafted the **Declaration of Independence**, signed July 4, 1776. The line that mattered: "all men are created equal." Jefferson owned slaves while writing it. The contradiction would define the next two centuries.
- **George Washington** led a ragged Continental Army that lost most of its battles but kept the war alive. The French alliance in 1778 turned the tide. **Victory at Yorktown in 1781** trapped the British army. The **Treaty of Paris (1783)** made it official: independence.

## Constitution and early republic (1787-1860)

- The first US constitution, the Articles of Confederation, was too weak. In 1787 delegates met in Philadelphia and wrote a new one. **James Madison** is called the "Father of the Constitution." It created a federal government with three branches and a system of checks and balances.
- The **Bill of Rights (1791)** added the first 10 amendments. Free speech, free press, right to bear arms, protections against the state.
- The country exploded westward across the 1800s. Louisiana Purchase in 1803 doubled the size. Wars with Mexico took the Southwest. The Trail of Tears in the 1830s forcibly removed tens of thousands of Native Americans.
- The whole time, the unresolved fight was slavery. The North industrialized. The South stayed agricultural and enslaved roughly 4 million people by 1860. Every compromise just delayed the reckoning.

## Civil War and Reconstruction (1861-1877)

- **Abraham Lincoln** won the 1860 presidency without a single Southern state. Eleven Southern states seceded. War broke out in 1861.
- The **Civil War (1861-1865)** killed roughly **750,000 Americans**, the bloodiest war in US history by far. The Union won. Slavery was abolished by the 13th Amendment. Lincoln was assassinated five days after the war ended.
- **Reconstruction** tried to integrate Black Americans into full citizenship. It worked briefly, then federal troops withdrew in 1877. Southern states immediately passed **Jim Crow** laws enforcing segregation that would last almost a century.

## Industrial age and immigration (1870-1914)

- The decades after the Civil War were the **Gilded Age**. Railroads, steel, oil, finance. Industrialists like **Andrew Carnegie** (steel) and **John D. Rockefeller** (oil) built fortunes that dwarfed anything in American history. Critics called them robber barons.
- Roughly **20 million immigrants** arrived between 1880 and 1920, mostly from Italy, Ireland, Eastern Europe, and Jewish communities fleeing pogroms. Ellis Island opened in 1892. The country became visibly polyglot.
- Inequality was extreme. The Progressive movement responded with antitrust laws, food safety regulation, and the income tax.

## World wars, depression, postwar boom (1914-1968)

- The US entered **World War I in 1917** late and tipped the war to the Allies. Then retreated into isolationism.
- The **Roaring 20s** were a boom of jazz, radio, cars, and stock speculation. It ended on **Black Tuesday in October 1929**. The **Great Depression** followed. Unemployment hit 25 percent.
- **Franklin Roosevelt** won in 1932 and built the New Deal: Social Security, federal jobs programs, banking reform. He won four elections. No one has done that since.
- **Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941)** dragged the US into World War II. The US ended the Pacific war by dropping atomic bombs on **Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945**. It emerged the dominant world power.
- The **Cold War** ran from 1947 to 1991. Marshall Plan rebuilt Europe. Korean War 1950-53 ended in stalemate. Vietnam War 1965-73 was the great failure. The fall of the **Berlin Wall in 1989** and the Soviet collapse in 1991 ended it.

## Unipolar America to polarization (1991-today)

- The 1990s were the "unipolar moment." Sole superpower, booming economy, the internet emerging. Bill Clinton presided over a budget surplus.
- **September 11, 2001** changed everything. The US invaded Afghanistan that October and Iraq in 2003. Both wars lasted 20 years and ended without clear victory.
- The **2008 financial crash** wiped out trillions in household wealth, exposed Wall Street recklessness, and seeded the populist anger of the next decade.
- **Barack Obama** won in 2008, the first Black president. **Donald Trump** won in 2016 as an outsider populist. **Joe Biden** beat Trump in 2020. Trump returned in 2024. The era is defined by deep polarization, distrust of institutions, and culture war.

## What to carry away

- The big through-line: a country founded on a contradiction (universal equality plus slavery) has spent four centuries working that contradiction out, expanding outward and inward at the same time.
- Every major contemporary American fight, race, federal power, guns, immigration, religion in public life, was already a fight in 1787.
- The US has reinvented itself roughly every 80 years: founding, Civil War and Reconstruction, New Deal and WWII, and now whatever this current realignment turns out to be.
- In one sentence: American history is the story of an experiment that kept expanding its definition of who counts, and is still arguing about it.
