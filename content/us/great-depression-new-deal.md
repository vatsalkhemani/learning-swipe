# The Great Depression and New Deal: 1929-1939, when American capitalism almost died

## What it was and why it matters

The Great Depression was the worst economic collapse in modern American history, running roughly 1929 to 1939. Unemployment hit around 25 percent. GDP fell by about 30 percent. Around 9,000 banks failed. The federal government's response, **Franklin Delano Roosevelt's** New Deal, rebuilt the financial system and created institutions like **Social Security** and the **FDIC** that still define American life. Every modern argument about the role of government in the economy traces back to this decade. Liberals see it as proof the federal government can save the country. Conservatives see it as the origin of the bloated administrative state. Both sides argue inside the frame the New Deal set up.

## How the crash happened

- The **1920s** were the Roaring Twenties. Stock market mania, easy credit, consumer goods on installment, jazz, prohibition, and a widespread belief that prosperity was permanent.
- Only around 3 percent of Americans owned stocks, but many borrowed heavily on margin, sometimes 90 cents on the dollar. When prices fell, those positions blew up fast.
- **Black Thursday, October 24, 1929**, then **Black Tuesday, October 29**. Markets crashed roughly 25 percent in two days, around 40 percent by mid-November.
- The stock losses were not the main problem. **Bank failures were.** Between 1929 and 1933 around 9,000 banks failed, roughly 40 percent of all U.S. banks. There was no federal deposit insurance. Depositors lost everything.
- **Smoot-Hawley Tariff, 1930.** Raised tariffs around 20 percent. Trade partners retaliated. Global trade collapsed, deepening the crisis worldwide.
- Unemployment hit around 25 percent by 1933. In some cities it ran over 50 percent. GDP fell roughly 30 percent between 1929 and 1933.

## Hoover and the wait

- **Herbert Hoover** (president 1929-33) believed in voluntarism: companies should hold wages steady, charities should feed the hungry, the federal government should stay mostly out.
- It didn't work. Shantytowns of homeless families sprang up in cities and were nicknamed **"Hoovervilles."**
- Hoover was not idle, he just acted small relative to the scale of the collapse. The voter judgment in 1932 was brutal.

## FDR and the New Deal

- **Franklin Delano Roosevelt** won the November 1932 election in a landslide. Democrat. Former governor of New York. Paralyzed from polio since 1921, which the press largely hid.
- **Inauguration, March 4, 1933.** "The only thing we have to fear is fear itself."
- **The First Hundred Days** passed 15 major bills. The pace is still the benchmark by which every new administration measures itself.

### First New Deal (1933)
- **CCC (Civilian Conservation Corps)** put young unemployed men into conservation projects (planted around 3 billion trees, built park infrastructure).
- **TVA (Tennessee Valley Authority)** built dams and electrified one of the poorest regions in the country.
- **FDIC (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation)** insured bank deposits. Bank runs essentially ended. Still operating.
- **SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)** regulated stock markets.
- **AAA (Agricultural Adjustment Act)** paid farmers to reduce production and stabilize prices.
- **NRA (National Recovery Administration)** set industry-wide codes for wages, hours, and prices. Later struck down by the Supreme Court.

### Second New Deal (1935)
- **Social Security Act.** Old-age pensions and unemployment insurance. The bedrock of the American social safety net. Still operating.
- **Wagner Act.** Legal protection for labor unions. Created the modern American labor movement.
- **WPA (Works Progress Administration).** Built around 650,000 miles of roads, around 125,000 buildings, employed around 8 million people over its life. Also paid artists, writers, photographers, and musicians to document the country.

## The court-packing crisis

- The Supreme Court struck down major New Deal laws in 1935 and 1936.
- **1937:** FDR proposed adding up to six new Supreme Court justices, effectively packing the court.
- It failed politically. Democrats in Congress would not go that far.
- But "the switch in time that saved nine": the Court started upholding New Deal legislation, possibly to head off the threat.

## Did it work?

- Debated. Unemployment was still around 14 percent in 1940. Full recovery came with **World War II spending**.
- But: the financial system was stabilized, deflation was stopped, mass starvation was averted, and the institutional framework that defined the country for the next 50 years was set.

## The New Deal coalition

- FDR built a Democratic coalition of **urban workers, Southern whites, Catholics, Jews, Black Americans, and intellectuals.**
- This coalition dominated American politics from 1932 to roughly 1968. Three of the four Democratic presidents between 1932 and 1968 (FDR, Truman, Kennedy, Johnson) all governed from inside it.
- It cracked over civil rights. When **Lyndon Johnson** signed the Civil Rights Act in 1964, he reportedly told an aide "we have lost the South for a generation." He was right.

## What people get wrong

- The crash didn't cause the Depression alone. The crash plus bank failures plus the contraction of money supply plus Smoot-Hawley plus the Federal Reserve raising rates at exactly the wrong moment is the real cause story. Economists still argue about the weighting.
- The New Deal wasn't socialism. It saved American capitalism by stabilizing it. Actual socialists at the time complained that it didn't go far enough.
- Recovery was not complete in 1939. World War II finished the recovery. The New Deal stopped the bleeding.

## Why it still matters

- **Social Security** and **FDIC** are still the spine of American economic life.
- Every modern American economic policy debate references New Deal precedents.
- Modern progressive politics traces itself to FDR, often explicitly (Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren).
- Conservatives critique the New Deal as the origin of an overgrown federal government, and have spent eighty years trying to roll it back without quite being able to.
- The bipartisan postwar consensus (1945 to roughly 1980) was a New Deal consensus. Both Democrats and Republicans accepted its core institutions. When that consensus broke in the **Reagan era**, what broke was the New Deal frame.

## What to carry away

- The country's economy nearly collapsed, the federal government built an entirely new institutional layer in response, and the country has been arguing about that layer ever since.
- The two pieces that have outlasted every political shift are **Social Security** and **deposit insurance**. Both are New Deal. Both are now untouchable.
- In one sentence: the New Deal saved American capitalism by changing what the federal government was for, and the country has been arguing about that change for four generations.
