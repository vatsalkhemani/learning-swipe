# The 1960s: the decade that broke American consensus

## What the decade was and why it matters

The 1960s were the pivot decade in modern American life. The country started it in confident postwar prosperity and finished it in fracture, with the civil rights movement having dismantled legal segregation, with around 58,000 American dead in Vietnam, with three of its most important leaders shot, and with a counterculture that rewrote norms around sex, drugs, and authority. Every American culture war fight since traces back to this decade. Race, gender, sexuality, religion, foreign wars, protest tactics, the trust in government question: all of it has a 1960s template. Boomers came of age inside it, and they have shaped the country's politics ever since.

## Civil rights

- **Greensboro sit-ins, February 1960.** Four Black college students sat at a Woolworth's lunch counter in North Carolina and refused to leave. Within months, sit-ins spread to 55 cities.
- **Freedom Rides, 1961.** Integrated groups rode interstate buses across the South to test desegregation rulings. Mobs attacked them. Buses were firebombed in Alabama.
- **Birmingham, 1963.** Police commissioner **Bull Connor** turned fire hoses and police dogs on Black children. The footage went national and global, and turned public opinion.
- **March on Washington, August 28, 1963.** Around 250,000 attendees. **Martin Luther King Jr.** delivered the "I Have a Dream" speech from the Lincoln Memorial steps.
- **Civil Rights Act, July 1964.** Banned discrimination in public accommodations and employment. Signed by **Lyndon Johnson**.
- **Voting Rights Act, August 1965.** Ended literacy tests and put Southern voting under federal oversight. Black registration in the Deep South jumped within a year.
- **Malcolm X assassinated, February 21, 1965.** Shot in Harlem.
- **MLK assassinated, April 4, 1968.** Shot at the Lorraine Motel in Memphis by **James Earl Ray**.
- **Black Power.** **Stokely Carmichael** coined the slogan in 1966. The **Black Panthers**, founded in Oakland in 1966 by **Huey Newton** and **Bobby Seale**, ran armed patrols and community programs, and clashed with the FBI.

## Vietnam

- U.S. ground troops escalated sharply in **1965** under **Lyndon Johnson**: **Operation Rolling Thunder** bombing campaign, around 184,000 troops by year-end.
- By 1968 there were around **500,000 American troops** in Vietnam.
- **Tet Offensive, January 1968.** North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces launched coordinated attacks across South Vietnam. They lost militarily but won psychologically: it shattered the official line that the war was nearly won. Walter Cronkite's broadcast skepticism after Tet is often pointed to as the moment middle America turned.
- **Anti-war movement exploded** on college campuses. Draft card burnings, sit-ins, the **1968 Democratic National Convention** in Chicago broke into open street violence.
- **Kent State, May 4, 1970.** Ohio National Guard troops fired on student protesters, killing four.
- **My Lai massacre**, committed March 1968, exposed publicly in 1969. U.S. soldiers killed hundreds of unarmed Vietnamese civilians. It collapsed the moral case for the war.
- **Pentagon Papers, 1971.** **Daniel Ellsberg** leaked classified Defense Department history showing successive administrations had lied about the war's progress.
- The war ended with American withdrawal in 1973 and **Saigon's fall in April 1975**. Around 58,000 Americans and an estimated 3 million Vietnamese died.

## The counterculture

- **Hippies, Summer of Love 1967** in San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury. Communal living, anti-materialism, psychedelic culture.
- **Woodstock, August 1969.** Around 400,000 people in upstate New York for three days of music. The closing image of the era's idealism.
- **Drug culture:** LSD via **Timothy Leary's** "tune in, turn on, drop out," marijuana mainstreamed for a generation.
- **Sexual revolution.** The Pill was approved by the FDA in 1960. Premarital sex, cohabitation, and divorce all rose sharply across the decade.
- **The music:** the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, **Bob Dylan**, **Jimi Hendrix**, **Motown**, Aretha Franklin, Janis Joplin. A small number of years produced an outsize share of the songs people still play.

## The political assassinations

- **John F. Kennedy, November 22, 1963.** Shot in Dallas by **Lee Harvey Oswald**. Trauma for an entire generation. Conspiracy theories started immediately.
- **Malcolm X, February 21, 1965.** Shot in Harlem.
- **MLK, April 4, 1968.** Shot in Memphis.
- **Robert F. Kennedy, June 6, 1968.** Shot in Los Angeles by **Sirhan Sirhan** the night he won the California primary. He was the leading Democratic candidate.
- Three Kennedy-linked shocks plus the assassination of the country's leading civil rights figure inside five years. The decade's emotional weight is hard to overstate.

## Other movements the decade started

- **Stonewall riots, June 1969.** Patrons of the Stonewall Inn, a New York gay bar, fought back against a police raid. Conventionally marked as the start of the modern LGBTQ rights movement.
- **Women's liberation.** **Betty Friedan's** *The Feminine Mystique* (1963) named "the problem that has no name." *Roe v. Wade* came in 1973 out of this organizing.
- **Environmental movement.** **Rachel Carson's** *Silent Spring* (1962) on pesticides launched it. The first **Earth Day** was April 22, 1970.

## The political realignment

- **1968 election.** **Richard Nixon** won by running on "law and order" and the **Southern Strategy**, using racial backlash to flip Southern white voters from Democrat to Republican.
- The realignment was real and durable. The Solid South, Democratic for a hundred years after the Civil War, became Republican by 1980, and has stayed largely Republican since.
- Johnson reportedly told an aide after signing the Civil Rights Act in 1964 that "we have lost the South for a generation." He was right.

## What people get wrong

- The decade was not just hippies and protests. Most Americans were not at Woodstock. **Nixon's 1968 win** and his 1972 reelection by landslide say a lot about the country: a substantial majority was reacting against the counterculture, not joining it.
- "The Sixties" as a cultural unit really runs roughly 1963 to 1974, from JFK's assassination through Watergate. The early '60s still looked like the 1950s. The late '60s and early '70s did the real cultural work.
- The civil rights movement did not start in 1960. It built on decades of prior NAACP work, on Brown v. Board (1954), on the Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955). The '60s were the breakthrough, not the beginning.

## Why it still matters

- Every modern culture war fight, abortion, guns, race, gender, the role of the courts, traces to 1960s arguments.
- The **Boomer generation** came of age inside the decade and have shaped American politics ever since.
- **Trust in government** never recovered from Vietnam and Watergate. The drop in trust shown in polls from the early '70s onward is one of the most consistent findings in American political science.
- The legal architecture built in the decade (Civil Rights Act, Voting Rights Act, *Roe*) is still litigated today.

## What to carry away

- The country entered the decade believing it had figured itself out and finished it knowing it hadn't.
- Three assassinations, a lost war, an emancipation moment, and a counterculture shift, all inside one decade.
- In one sentence: the 1960s ended the postwar American consensus and replaced it with the unresolved fights that still define American politics.
