# Modern literature: the authors and books defining the last 50 years

## What this canon is and why it matters

The contemporary literary canon, roughly the work of the last 50 years, looks dramatically different from the 20th-century canon that most school syllabi still teach. Postcolonial voices moved to the center. Latin America's magical realism shaped a generation of writers worldwide. Japanese and Korean fiction crossed into mainstream English readership. Toni Morrison, not Hemingway, is the dominant American literary reference. Knowing this canon matters because literature is still how educated people reference each other and how cultural conversation organizes itself. Reading the contemporary works = participating in the conversation that is already happening.

## The Latin American Boom and after

- **Gabriel García Márquez** (Colombian). **One Hundred Years of Solitude (1967)** is magical realism's defining text. He won the Nobel in 1982.
- **Mario Vargas Llosa** (Peru, Nobel 2010), **Carlos Fuentes** (Mexico), and **Julio Cortázar** (Argentina) were the other Boom giants.
- **Roberto Bolaño** (Chilean) became huge posthumously. **The Savage Detectives** and the unfinished **2666** are essential.
- **Isabel Allende** (Chile) carried magical realism into the popular mainstream worldwide.

## Postcolonial literature, the most important category

- **Chinua Achebe** (Nigerian). **Things Fall Apart (1958)** is the most-read African novel.
- **Wole Soyinka** (Nigerian) won the Nobel in 1986, the first African winner.
- **Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o** (Kenyan) is the major anticolonial voice, eventually writing in Gikuyu rather than English on principle.
- **J.M. Coetzee** (South African) won the Nobel in 2003. **Disgrace** and **Waiting for the Barbarians** are the entry points.
- **Salman Rushdie** (Indian-British). **Midnight's Children (1981)** won the Booker. **The Satanic Verses (1988)** drew Khomeini's fatwa, and he survived a stabbing in New York in 2022.
- **Arundhati Roy** (Indian). **The God of Small Things (1997)** won the Booker.
- **V.S. Naipaul** (Trinidad-British) won the Nobel in 2001. **A House for Mr Biswas** is the masterpiece. A politically contentious figure.

## Asian literature in English translation

- **Haruki Murakami** (Japanese) is the global cult phenomenon. **Norwegian Wood**, **Kafka on the Shore**, and **1Q84**.
- **Kenzaburō Ōe** (Nobel 1994) and **Yoko Ogawa** carried the Japanese tradition with less mainstream attention but high critical regard.
- **Kazuo Ishiguro** (Japanese-British, Nobel 2017). **The Remains of the Day** and **Never Let Me Go** are the canonical works.
- **Mo Yan** (Chinese, Nobel 2012) is internationally important but politically contentious, seen as an establishment figure.
- **Ha Jin** and **Yiyun Li** are the major Chinese-American voices.

## American literature after 1970

- **Toni Morrison**. **Beloved (1987)** and **Song of Solomon**. Pulitzer winner and Nobel laureate in 1993, the first African-American woman to win the literature Nobel. The dominant reference of the modern American canon.
- **Cormac McCarthy**. **Blood Meridian (1985)**, **No Country for Old Men (2005)**, **The Road (2006)**.
- **Don DeLillo**. **White Noise** and **Underworld**.
- **Philip Roth**. **American Pastoral** and **Portnoy's Complaint**.
- **John Updike**. The Rabbit tetralogy is the long American suburban epic.
- **David Foster Wallace**. **Infinite Jest (1996)** became a generational text.
- **Jonathan Franzen**. **The Corrections** and **Freedom**.
- Modern American also: **George Saunders** (**Lincoln in the Bardo**) and **Marilynne Robinson** (**Gilead**).

## The recent generation, roughly 2010 onward

- **Sally Rooney** (Irish). **Normal People (2018)** and **Conversations with Friends**.
- **Ocean Vuong** (Vietnamese-American). Poet and novelist, **On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous**.
- **Ta-Nehisi Coates**. **Between the World and Me** reshaped the essay-as-letter form.
- **Yaa Gyasi**. **Homegoing** is the multigenerational Ghanaian-American epic.
- **Hanya Yanagihara**. **A Little Life (2015)** became a cult phenomenon.
- **Min Jin Lee**. **Pachinko (2017)** is the multigenerational Korean-Japanese epic.

## What's changed about the canon

- It's far more global. The Nobel and Booker shortlists have been steadily less Anglo-American and less male.
- Postcolonial writing is no longer a sub-category; it's a central tradition.
- The female canon is dramatically larger and more central than it was 50 years ago.
- Translation has become economically and culturally serious. Foreign-language literature now reaches Anglophone readers within months, not decades.

## What people get wrong

- Treating "literary fiction" as one thing. The Latin American Boom, the postcolonial wave, the Japanese mainstream, and the contemporary American canon are distinct traditions with different concerns and forms.
- Skipping translated work because of a habit. The most acclaimed novels of the last 30 years often weren't originally in English.
- Assuming the high school canon is the canon. Hemingway, Fitzgerald, and Steinbeck still matter, but they aren't where the contemporary conversation lives.

## What to carry away

- García Márquez, Morrison, and Rushdie are the three most-referenced figures of the modern era. Start there if you start anywhere.
- The contemporary canon is more global, more postcolonial, and more female than the one you were taught.
- In one sentence: modern literature still shapes how educated people think and reference each other, and the conversation is no longer centered in New York or London.
