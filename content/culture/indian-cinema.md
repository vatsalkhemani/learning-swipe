# Indian cinema: Bollywood, the South Indian wave, and parallel cinema

## What Indian cinema is and why it matters

Indian cinema is the world's largest film industry by output, producing roughly 2,000+ films a year across all its languages, of which around 1,500 are Hindi-language Bollywood films. It is not one industry but several, each with its own language, stars, aesthetics, and audience. The center of gravity has shifted twice in the last twenty years: first globalizing outward, then sliding south as Tamil and Telugu cinema overtook Bollywood at the box office. Indian cinema matters because it shapes fashion, music, language, and politics across roughly 1.8 billion South Asians plus a large global diaspora, and increasingly it influences global audiences directly.

## Hindi cinema (Bollywood)

- Based in **Mumbai**. Origins go back to **1913**, when **Dadasaheb Phalke** released **Raja Harishchandra**, India's first silent feature.
- The **Golden Age** ran through the 1950s and 60s. **Raj Kapoor's Awara (1951)** became a massive hit in the Soviet Union. **Mehboob Khan's Mother India (1957)** was Oscar-nominated. **Mughal-e-Azam (1960)** is still cited as one of the most influential Indian films ever made.
- The 1970s belonged to **Amitabh Bachchan** and the "Angry Young Man" era. **Sholay (1975)** is still re-released in theaters regularly.
- The 1990s opened the **Khan era**. **Shah Rukh Khan**, **Aamir Khan**, and **Salman Khan**, collectively the three Khans, dominated the industry for the next 30+ years. **Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995)** ran in one Mumbai theater continuously for around 30 years, a world record.
- The 2000s globalized Bollywood. **Lagaan (2001)** was Oscar-nominated. **Slumdog Millionaire (2008)** was a UK production but a Mumbai story, and it swept the Oscars.
- The modern era includes Aamir Khan's **PK**, **Dangal**, and **Secret Superstar**. Dangal made around $300M in China alone, where Aamir became a phenomenon. Shah Rukh Khan's **Pathaan** and **Jawan (2023)** marked his comeback after a four-year break.
- The two dominant production houses are **Karan Johar's Dharma Productions** and **Yash Raj Films**.

## South Indian cinema, the wave that overtook Bollywood

- Through the 2010s and 2020s, South Indian films have outpaced Bollywood at the box office and increasingly globally.
- **Telugu cinema (Tollywood)** is led by director **S.S. Rajamouli**, whose **Baahubali (2015 and 2017)** broke all Indian box office records. **RRR (2022)** went further: its song **Naatu Naatu** won the Oscar for Best Original Song, the biggest Telugu hit globally.
- **Tamil cinema (Kollywood)** has the deepest star culture. **Rajinikanth** is a cult figure whose film openings are ritualistic events. **Kamal Haasan** is the versatile actor's actor. **Vijay**, known as Thalapathy, is the current mass-market king. Modern hits include **Vikram**, **Master**, and **Beast**.
- **Malayalam cinema (Mollywood)** is the most critically acclaimed of all Indian cinemas. **Drishyam (2013, with a 2021 sequel)** was so good it got a Hindi remake. **The Great Indian Kitchen (2021)** is a sharp feminist critique. **Mohanlal** and **Mammootty** have been megastars for 40+ years.
- **Kannada cinema** is rising too. **KGF: Chapter 1 (2018) and Chapter 2 (2022)** broke through pan-India, and **Kantara (2022)** followed.

## Parallel cinema (the art-house tradition)

- **Satyajit Ray** is the giant. His **Pather Panchali (1955)** and the rest of the **Apu Trilogy** are critically acclaimed worldwide and won at Cannes.
- **Ritwik Ghatak** worked in Bengali, more obscure but deeply influential on later filmmakers.
- **Mrinal Sen**, **Shyam Benegal**, and **Govind Nihalani** carried the tradition forward through the 70s and 80s.
- Modern parallel cinema includes **Court (Marathi, 2014)**, **Masaan (Hindi, 2015)**, and **Sairat (Marathi, 2016)**, which became the biggest Marathi hit ever.

## Why Indian cinema matters globally

- Largest film market in the world by audience.
- A distinct aesthetic vocabulary: song-and-dance sequences, family melodrama, hero worship as a structural element, not a flaw.
- Cultural influence flows out across fashion, music, language, and politics in South Asia and the diaspora.
- The **streaming era** (Netflix India, Amazon Prime Video, Disney+ Hotstar, JioCinema) is accelerating both variety and reach. The non-Bollywood Indian industries have been the biggest beneficiaries.

## What people get wrong

- Calling all Indian cinema "Bollywood." Bollywood is only the Hindi-language Mumbai industry. The Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Bengali, and Marathi industries are separate, and several of them now out-earn Bollywood.
- Treating song-and-dance as filler. In the Indian tradition, the song sequences are often where character interiority happens, not the dialogue.
- Assuming Indian cinema is just for Indian audiences. **Aamir Khan in China**, **RRR at the Oscars**, **Bollywood across the Gulf and Africa**, the diaspora theaters across the US and UK — the global footprint is large and growing.

## What to carry away

- Indian cinema is the largest film industry in the world by output, not one industry but many.
- Bollywood is the most famous, but the South Indian wave has overtaken it in recent years.
- Satyajit Ray remains the global art-house reference; modern parallel cinema is alive in Marathi, Malayalam, and Hindi.
- In one sentence: Indian cinema is the largest film ecosystem on earth, in the middle of a center-of-gravity shift southward, and increasingly shaping global pop culture.
