# US sports basics: the calendar, the leagues, and how to fake it at any dinner party

## What US sports is and why it matters

Sports is the default small talk of American adult life. Offices open Mondays with "did you watch the game." Dinner parties drift to it when politics gets uncomfortable. Weekend plans get built around it. If you moved here from a country where football means soccer and cricket runs for five days, the rules are confusing and the seasons overlap and nobody slows down to explain. The good news: there are only four leagues that matter, the calendar is almost always running, and you can be functional in any conversation by learning the basics of one team in your city. Below is what's actually worth knowing.

## The calendar

- **NFL** (American football) runs late August to early February. Sunday is the ritual day. Monday Night Football and Thursday Night Football fill the rest. The Super Bowl in early February is the single biggest TV event of the year in America, usually pulling 100M+ viewers.
- **NBA** (basketball) runs October to mid-June. Games happen most nights. Christmas Day games are a marquee tradition. The Finals stretch into June.
- **MLB** (baseball) runs April to early November. 162 regular-season games per team. It is the slowest sport with the longest grind. The World Series ends in late October or early November.
- **NHL** (hockey) runs October to mid-June. Less culturally central than the others, much bigger in the Northeast, Midwest, and Canada. Stanley Cup Finals overlap with the NBA Finals.

The practical takeaway: from late August through June there is almost always a major league mid-season. Only July is quiet, and even then baseball is grinding.

## NFL: the biggest one

- **32 teams**, split into two conferences (AFC and NFC). 17-game regular season. The playoffs are single-elimination, which is why each game feels enormous.
- **Super Bowl** is in early February. The halftime show and commercials are nearly as discussed as the game itself.
- **The Patriots dynasty (2001-2018)** under quarterback **Tom Brady** and coach **Bill Belichick** won 6 Super Bowls. The reference point for sustained excellence in the modern era.
- **The Chiefs dynasty (2019-present)** with quarterback **Patrick Mahomes** is the current version. Three Super Bowls in five years.
- **Salary cap and revenue sharing** are why small markets like Green Bay and Kansas City can compete with New York. The league forces a level financial playing field, which is why Americans take the NFL more seriously as a "fair" sport than baseball.
- Teams to know in major cities: **Patriots** (Boston), **Giants** and **Jets** (NYC), **Cowboys** (Dallas, the most-watched team in the country regardless of record), **49ers** (San Francisco), **Eagles** (Philadelphia), **Bears** (Chicago), **Steelers** (Pittsburgh).

## NBA: the global one

- **30 teams**, **82 regular-season games**. The playoffs are best-of-seven series across four rounds.
- The historic anchors are **Los Angeles Lakers**, **Boston Celtics**, **Chicago Bulls**, **NY Knicks**. The **Lakers-Celtics rivalry** runs through the entire history of the league.
- Current era stars worth knowing: **LeBron James** (the modern reference, now 21 seasons in), **Stephen Curry** (Golden State, changed how the game is played by making the 3-point shot central), **Kevin Durant**, **Nikola Jokic** (Serbian, Denver, two-time MVP), **Luka Doncic** (Slovenian, Dallas then LA, the next face of the league).
- The NBA has the strongest international presence of any US league. Top players come from Serbia, Slovenia, Greece, Cameroon, Australia, France. Half the All-Star team in a given year may be non-American.
- Bigger in big cities and on coasts. Less rural penetration than NFL or MLB.

## MLB: the slow one with the deepest roots

- **30 teams**, **162-game season**, which is brutal and also the point. Baseball fans love the daily-ritual nature of it.
- The most-followed franchises are the **NY Yankees** (27 World Series titles, the most in any North American sport), **LA Dodgers** (current spending leader, signed Shohei Ohtani in 2023), **Boston Red Sox** (their 86-year championship drought ending in 2004 is a foundational sports story).
- Baseball lost ground to the NFL and NBA in cultural centrality since the 90s. But it has the deepest regional fan bases. A Yankees fan or a Cardinals fan is a Yankees fan or a Cardinals fan for life.
- The pace is slow by design. Three hours of pitcher vs batter chess with occasional explosions. Recent rule changes (pitch clock, bigger bases) sped it up.

## NHL: the niche one

- **32 teams**, mostly in northern US cities and Canada. Fast, physical, high-skill.
- Stanley Cup playoffs are widely considered the most grueling in pro sports. Players grow playoff beards. They play through broken bones.
- Big in Boston, NYC, Philly, Detroit, Chicago, Pittsburgh, Minnesota, Colorado, Vegas. Not big in the South, with exceptions like Tampa Bay and Carolina.

## The non-pro side that matters more than you'd think

- **NCAA college football** is enormous, especially in the South and Midwest. **Alabama**, **Georgia**, **Ohio State**, **Michigan** are the historic powers. In states without an NFL team, college football is the dominant sport.
- **March Madness** is the NCAA basketball tournament every March. 68 teams, single elimination. Office bracket pools are nearly universal in American workplaces. Even people who don't watch basketball fill out a bracket.

## Soccer

- **MLS** (Major League Soccer) is the US league. **Lionel Messi** joining Inter Miami in 2023 made the biggest noise the league has ever had.
- Most American soccer fans actually follow the **Premier League** (England). Saturday mornings. Liverpool, Manchester City, Arsenal, the big names are familiar even to casual American fans.

## How to fake it

- Pick **one team per league** in your new city. Philly means Eagles, 76ers, Phillies, Flyers. Boston means Patriots, Celtics, Red Sox, Bruins. NYC means Giants or Jets, Knicks, Yankees or Mets, Rangers.
- Learn the last 3 years of headlines for those teams. Did they make the playoffs. Who's the star player. Did they fire the coach.
- Watch one game per league with a friend who explains. You'll catch the basics faster than reading rules online.
- If lost in conversation: "How did they look this week" works as a question in any sport. So does "is [player] healthy."

In one sentence: there are four major leagues, the seasons overlap so something is always on, and learning one team in your city is enough to stop being a tourist in American culture.
