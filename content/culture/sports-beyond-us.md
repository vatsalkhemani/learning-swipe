# Sports beyond US: soccer, cricket, F1, and the global games US barely follows

## Why this matters

The American sports conversation (NFL, NBA, MLB) is a domestic conversation. The global sports conversation is mostly soccer, cricket, and Formula 1, with rugby, tennis, and golf as the other significant categories. Roughly 96% of the world doesn't follow American sports the way Americans do. Your MBA classmates from London, Lagos, Mumbai, São Paulo, and Sydney almost certainly follow soccer or cricket more closely than the NBA. Knowing the contours of these games matters because sports talk is one of the universal social lubricants, and the global version of it is happening in a different vocabulary than the American one.

## Soccer (Football outside the US)

- The world's most popular sport. Roughly **4 billion fans** globally.
- The major European leagues:
  - **Premier League (England)**, around **$7B in annual revenue**, the most-watched globally. Historic top teams: **Manchester United**, **Liverpool**, **Arsenal**, **Chelsea**, **Manchester City**, **Tottenham**.
  - **La Liga (Spain)**: **Real Madrid** and **Barcelona** dominate the cultural footprint.
  - **Bundesliga (Germany)**: **Bayern Munich** has been dominant for over a decade.
  - **Serie A (Italy)**: historic depth, with **Juventus** the modern dynasty.
  - **Ligue 1 (France)**: **PSG** has dominated since Qatari ownership.
- The **UEFA Champions League** is the annual European club competition. **Real Madrid** has a record **15 titles**.
- The **FIFA World Cup**, held every 4 years, is the biggest single sporting event in the world (roughly **5 billion viewers** across the tournament). Recent winners: **Spain 2010**, **Germany 2014**, **France 2018**, **Argentina 2022** (the tournament where **Messi** finally won the trophy).
- The major players today:
  - **Lionel Messi** (Argentine), **8 Ballons d'Or**, the most ever.
  - **Cristiano Ronaldo** (Portuguese), **5 Ballons d'Or**.
  - **Erling Haaland** (Norwegian striker, Manchester City).
  - **Kylian Mbappé** (French, now at Real Madrid).
- The **Saudi Pro League** signed **Ronaldo** and **Benzema** in 2023, part of a sustained Saudi push into global sport that critics call sportswashing.

## Cricket

- Dominant in former British colonies. Roughly **2.5 billion fans**, the second-largest sport globally.
- Three formats matter:
  - **Test cricket**: 5 days, the traditional format.
  - **ODI (One Day International)**: 50 overs per side, the **Cricket World Cup** format.
  - **T20**: roughly 3 hours, modernized for TV.
- **India is cricket's economic superpower.** The **Indian Premier League (IPL)**, founded **2008**, revolutionized the sport. It's the most-watched and most-lucrative cricket league, with franchise auctions valued over **$1B**. Top franchises: **Mumbai Indians**, **Chennai Super Kings**, **Royal Challengers Bangalore**.
- The major players today:
  - **Virat Kohli** (Indian), the modern face of cricket globally.
  - **MS Dhoni** (Indian, retired captain, IPL legend).
  - **Steve Smith** and **Pat Cummins** (Australians).
  - **Joe Root** and **Ben Stokes** (English).
  - **Babar Azam** (Pakistani).
- **India vs Pakistan** matches are the most-watched cricket events in the world. **Australia vs England** (the **Ashes**) is the historic rivalry.

## Formula 1

- Open-wheel motorsport, 20 to 24 races per year held across the world. Roughly **$2.5B** in annual revenue.
- The major drivers:
  - **Lewis Hamilton** (British), **7 world titles**, tied for the all-time record.
  - **Max Verstappen** (Dutch), dominant from 2021 through 2024.
  - **Charles Leclerc** and **Lando Norris** are the next-generation contenders.
- The major teams: **Red Bull** (Honda engines), **Mercedes**, **Ferrari** (the sentimental favorite, the Italian national team in motorsport terms).
- Netflix's **Drive to Survive** (2019 onward) quintupled the US audience for F1 and is one of the cleanest case studies of a sport buying its way into a new market through narrative content.

## Other major global sports

- **Rugby**: England, New Zealand, South Africa, Australia, France, and Argentina are the top tier. The **Rugby World Cup** runs every 4 years.
- **Tennis**: the four Grand Slams (**Wimbledon**, **US Open**, **Australian Open**, **French Open**) anchor the calendar. **Federer**, **Nadal**, and **Djokovic** each won 20+ slams. The current generation is **Carlos Alcaraz** and **Jannik Sinner**.
- **Golf**: the **PGA Tour** is US-centric. The Saudi-funded **LIV Golf** split fractured the sport in the early 2020s. The four majors (**Masters**, **PGA Championship**, **US Open**, **Open Championship**) still organize the year.
- **MMA / UFC**: global audience but mostly Anglosphere stars. **Conor McGregor**, **Khabib Nurmagomedov**, **Israel Adesanya**.
- **The Olympics** every 2 years, alternating Summer and Winter. The one event where the US sports audience overlaps cleanly with the global one.

## What people get wrong

- Treating the NFL or NBA as global sports. They're growing internationally but still nowhere close to soccer or cricket in fan base outside North America.
- Calling soccer boring because of low scores. The whole structure of the game (90 minutes of continuous play, no commercial breaks, scoring rare enough that goals feel earned) is built for a different rhythm.
- Underestimating cricket. The IPL alone has the revenue and audience size of multiple US leagues combined.

## What to carry away

- Soccer is the only true global sport, with roughly 4 billion fans.
- Cricket is second, anchored by the Indian market and the IPL.
- F1 has bought its way back into US relevance through Netflix and is one of the cleanest growth stories in sport.
- In one sentence: most of the world's sports conversation happens in soccer, cricket, and F1, not the American leagues.
