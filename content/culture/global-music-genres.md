# Global music: beyond Anglo-American pop, the genres conquering streaming

## What this shift is and why it matters

For most of the 20th century, pop music meant Anglo-American pop music. The Beatles, Madonna, Michael Jackson, Beyoncé. That assumption broke in the 2010s. Today the most-streamed artist on Spotify in multiple years has been a Puerto Rican rapper recording entirely in Spanish. The biggest band in the world performs primarily in Korean. African pop is mainstream in London and New York. This isn't a fad. It's structural, driven by streaming economics and algorithmic recommendation that flattened distribution. If you grew up assuming the global pop charts belonged to English, that assumption is now wrong, and your future colleagues from Lagos, Seoul, Bogotá, and Mumbai grew up on different music than you did.

## K-pop, the engineered juggernaut

- South Korean pop music as an industrial system. The roots go back to **Seo Taiji and Boys (1992)**, the band that imported hip-hop and rock into mainstream Korean pop.
- The model exploded globally in the 2010s. Labels recruit trainees young, sometimes from age 10, and train them in vocals, dance, languages, and on-camera presence for years before debut. Groups are constructed deliberately, with visual, vocal, and dance roles distributed across members.
- **BTS** (RM, Jin, Suga, J-Hope, Jimin, V, Jungkook, formed 2013) broke through globally, set chart records, performed at the UN, and by 2020 was arguably the biggest band in the world.
- **Blackpink**, a four-member girl group, headlined Coachella.
- The next generation includes **Stray Kids**, **NewJeans**, **Le Sserafim**, and **IVE**.
- K-pop generates roughly $10B+ for the South Korean economy and gives the country soft-power diplomatic weight far beyond its size.

## Latin music, the Spanish-language takeover

- **Reggaetón** originated in Panama in the 1990s and exploded out of Puerto Rico in the 2000s. **Daddy Yankee's Gasolina (2004)** was the first global reggaetón hit.
- **Bad Bunny** (Puerto Rico) became the most-streamed artist globally on Spotify multiple years in the 2020s, recording almost entirely in Spanish. **J Balvin**, **Karol G**, and **Rosalía** (Spanish flamenco-pop fusion) followed.
- **Despacito (2017)** by **Luis Fonsi and Daddy Yankee** was the first non-English song to become the most-streamed track of all time, a structural moment for the global charts.
- Adjacent genres are surging too: **Latin trap**, **Brazilian funk**, **cumbia**, **bachata**, and **vallenato** in modernized forms.

## Afrobeats, Africa's pop export

- Nigeria's pop ecosystem. **Fela Kuti's 1970s Afrobeat** (with the "t") laid the political and rhythmic groundwork. Modern Afrobeats (often without the "t") is its descendant.
- **D'banj** and **P-Square** broke through in the 2000s. The 2010s and 2020s explosion was led by **Wizkid** (his **Made in Lagos**, 2020, was globally acclaimed), **Burna Boy** (won a Grammy for **Twice as Tall**, 2020, after the breakthrough of **African Giant**), and **Davido**.
- **Tems** was featured on hits by Future, Drake, and Wizkid before going solo. **Ayra Starr** and **Tyla** (South African; her track **Water**, 2023, was a global hit) are the next wave.
- South Africa's **Amapiano** subgenre is crossing into Western markets right now.

## Other major non-Western pop ecosystems

- **Indian hip-hop**: **DIVINE** and **Naezy** were the foundation of the Mumbai underground that the 2019 film **Gully Boy** brought to the world.
- **J-pop and anime music**: long established domestically, now traveling globally. **Joe Hisaishi's Studio Ghibli scores** are concert sellouts in the West. Anime opening themes routinely go viral on TikTok.
- Latin folk genres modernized: **cumbia**, **bachata**, **vallenato** all have young pop artists reworking them.

## Why this is a structural shift, not a trend

- The 2010s peak of Anglo-American pop dominance is ending.
- **Spotify** levels distribution globally. Anyone in the world can release a track to every market simultaneously.
- Recommendation algorithms surface non-English music to Western listeners without requiring radio gatekeepers.
- Language is less of a barrier than it used to be. People stream songs they don't understand the lyrics of, and they sing along phonetically. **Bad Bunny** and **BTS** routinely top global charts entirely in non-English.
- In some US surveys, roughly half the music streamed is now non-English.

## What this means for you

- Cultural product is global now in a way it wasn't 15 years ago.
- Your MBA classmates from outside the US grew up listening to artists you've never heard of, and they expect that to be a normal conversation.
- The next century's pop superstars are unlikely to be predominantly American or British.

## What to carry away

- K-pop is engineered, factory-style, and globally dominant.
- Latin music, led by Bad Bunny and reggaetón, broke the English-only chart ceiling.
- Afrobeats made Nigerian pop a global category.
- The driver is Spotify-era distribution plus algorithmic recommendation, not a marketing wave.
- In one sentence: the global pop chart is no longer English-default, and the artists running it now grew up in Seoul, San Juan, and Lagos.
