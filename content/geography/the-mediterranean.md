# The Mediterranean: the civilization engine

## What the Mediterranean is and why it matters

The Mediterranean Sea covers roughly **2.5 million km²**, bordered by **22 countries** across **three continents** (Europe, Africa, Asia). It's nearly enclosed: the **Strait of Gibraltar** to the Atlantic, the **Suez Canal** to the Red Sea (since 1869), and the **Dardanelles** and **Bosphorus** to the Black Sea. That enclosure made it function as connective tissue, not a barrier. For about 3,000 years it was the most consequential body of water on Earth. The Phoenicians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Arabs, Ottomans, and modern Europeans all used it as their highway. Most of what we call Western civilization was written around its shores.

## Why this sea produced so much civilization

- Relatively calm compared to the Atlantic, navigable by small craft.
- An indented coast with many natural harbors.
- Islands as stepping stones across open water (Crete, Cyprus, Sicily, the Aegean).
- A **Mediterranean climate**: mild wet winters, dry warm summers. Olives, grapes, and wheat grow well. Wine and oil became export commodities millennia before they became dinner staples.
- Three continents meeting at one body of water means trade goods, ideas, and religions all moved through the same network.

## The Phoenicians and the alphabet (~1500-300 BCE)

- City-states (**Tyre**, **Sidon**, **Byblos**) on the coast of modern Lebanon.
- Greatest sailors of antiquity. Founded colonies across the Mediterranean: **Cádiz** in Spain, **Carthage** in Tunisia (founded ~814 BCE).
- Invented or popularized the **phonetic alphabet**, from which Greek, Latin, Arabic, Hebrew, and most European alphabets descend. Easier than cuneiform or hieroglyphics. Literacy spread.
- Their commercial empire was eventually destroyed when Rome flattened Carthage in 146 BCE.

## The Greeks (~800-146 BCE)

- Independent city-states: **Athens**, **Sparta**, **Corinth**, **Thebes**, and dozens more.
- A maritime empire of colonies stretched across Sicily, southern Italy ("Magna Graecia"), the southern coast of France (**Marseille** was founded by Greeks around 600 BCE), and the Black Sea.
- **Athens' Golden Age** in the 5th century BCE produced democracy, philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), theater (Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides), and history (Herodotus, Thucydides) in a single century.
- **Alexander the Great's** empire from 336-323 BCE spread Greek language and culture all the way to Egypt, Persia, and India. The Hellenistic period that followed kept Greek as a lingua franca for centuries.

## Rome and the "Our Sea" period (~509 BCE - 476 CE)

- Started as a single Italian peninsula city, expanded around the Mediterranean basin, and eventually controlled the entire coast. The Romans called it **"Mare Nostrum"** (our sea), and it was literally true.
- Produced Roman law (the foundation of most modern legal systems), engineering (aqueducts, roads, concrete), and Latin (from which Italian, Spanish, French, Portuguese, and Romanian descend).
- **Christianity** spread along Roman trade routes and eventually became the imperial religion under **Constantine** (313 CE).
- The **Pax Romana** (27 BCE - 180 CE) was about two centuries of relative peace and prosperity across the basin. Few periods in human history match it.

## The split and the contest with Islam

- Rome formally divided in **285 CE**.
- The **Western Roman Empire** fell in **476 CE** to Germanic tribes.
- The **Eastern Roman Empire** (Byzantine Empire) lasted another thousand years until **1453**, with Constantinople as capital.
- The **Islamic conquests** of the 7th and 8th centuries swept out of Arabia, captured Egypt, North Africa, and most of Spain within a hundred years.
- The Mediterranean became contested between Christendom and Islam.
- The **Crusades** (1096-1291) were a series of European attempts to retake the Holy Land. Mostly unsuccessful long-term, but they reconnected Europe to Mediterranean and Eastern trade.
- The **Spanish Reconquista** ended in **1492** with the fall of Granada, the same year Columbus sailed.
- **Ottoman dominance** from **1453** (the fall of Constantinople) until roughly **1700**.

## Why it stopped mattering for a while

- After 1492 the **Atlantic** became the central trade ocean. The Americas, India routes around Africa, and the rise of Spain, Portugal, the Netherlands, and Britain pulled commerce out of the Mediterranean.
- For about three centuries the Mediterranean was a backwater of European politics rather than its center.

## Why it matters again

- The **Suez Canal opened in 1869**, reviving the Mediterranean as the shortest route between Europe and Asia.
- Today around **30% of world shipping** passes through it.
- Current flashpoints:
  - **Migrant crisis**. The Mediterranean route has been the deadliest migration corridor in the world; thousands have drowned attempting it.
  - **Israel and Palestine**.
  - **Greek-Turkish tensions** over Cyprus and maritime borders.
  - **Russia's Mediterranean presence**, notably the naval base at **Tartus** in Syria.
- Climate change is hitting hard. The sea is warming faster than average, fish stocks are collapsing, water scarcity is intensifying around the southern rim, and wildfires have become annual events in Greece, Italy, Spain, and Turkey.

## What to carry away

- The Mediterranean's geography (enclosed sea, three continents, mild climate, harbors) made it the most consequential body of water in human history.
- Civilizations didn't develop here by accident. The map made it possible.
- Most of what English-speaking readers think of as "the West" was written on its shores by Phoenicians, Greeks, Romans, and the people who came after.
- It's contested again today, just with different actors.
- In one sentence: the Mediterranean is the sea where three continents meet, and the place where most of the world's old ideas were first written down.
