# Africa's geography: why one continent has 54 countries and the hardest development path

## What Africa's geography is and why it matters

Africa covers around **30 million km²**, the second-largest continent, and roughly **three times the size of Europe**. A common surprise comparison: the US, China, India, and most of Western Europe combined would fit inside it. The population is about **1.4 billion people** across **54 countries**, more than any other continent. The geography that defined Africa is harsh in ways that don't apply to Europe or Asia: a huge desert in the middle, rivers that don't help with trade, a tropical disease burden, and colonial borders that ignored every internal logic. Africa's century is starting now. To understand why it's been the hardest development path, you start with the map.

## The Sahara split

- The **Sahara** is the world's largest hot desert, around **9 million km²**.
- It splits North Africa (Arab and Berber, historically connected to the Mediterranean and Middle East) from Sub-Saharan Africa (Black African, historically more isolated).
- These two halves followed very different historical trajectories. North Africa was part of the Mediterranean world. Sub-Saharan Africa developed largely on its own until the era of trans-Saharan trade and then the Atlantic slave trade.
- The Sahara is also expanding south (desertification in the **Sahel**), pushing populations into smaller fertile zones and worsening conflict.

## The geographic problems Europe didn't have

- **Few natural harbors.** Africa's coastline is smoother than Europe's, with fewer of the indented bays and estuaries that produce great ports.
- **Rivers that don't help with trade.** The major rivers (**Niger, Congo, Nile, Zambezi**) have waterfalls and rapids close to the coast, making them hard to navigate inland from the ocean. The Nile is the exception, navigable from the Mediterranean deep into the interior, which is part of why ancient Egypt could exist.
- **Disease burden.** Malaria, sleeping sickness, schistosomiasis, and yellow fever devastated populations and made foreign occupation difficult. **Quinine** was the breakthrough that let Europeans colonize the interior after the 1880s.
- **Resource curse.** Africa holds vast mineral wealth: gold, diamonds, copper, and **cobalt** (the DRC holds about **70% of world cobalt reserves**, critical for batteries). Around 30% of the world's known mineral reserves sit on the continent. Extraction tends to enrich elites and foreign companies without broad development.

## Colonial borders and the cost they imposed

- The **1884-85 Berlin Conference** carved up Africa with rulers and minimal regard for the ethnic, linguistic, or geographic realities on the ground.
- Roughly **10,000 ethnic groups** and **2,000 languages** got forced into 54 states.
- This produced enduring conflicts:
  - **Hutu and Tutsi** in Rwanda and Burundi (precipitated the **1994 Rwandan Genocide**, ~800,000 dead in 100 days).
  - **Igbo, Yoruba, and Hausa** in Nigeria (precipitated the **Biafra War of 1967-70**).
  - Sudan, South Sudan, DRC, Ethiopia-Eritrea, Somalia, and many more.
- The borders weren't drawn for Africans. They were drawn for European negotiators in a room far away.

## Pre-colonial Africa was not a blank slate

- The colonial narrative implied Africa had no real history before Europeans arrived. False.
- Functioning kingdoms and empires existed for centuries:
  - **Mali Empire** (~1230-1670). **Mansa Musa** in the 14th century may have been the richest person in history.
  - **Songhai Empire** (~1340-1591).
  - **Great Zimbabwe** (~1100-1450), a stone city in southern Africa.
  - **Ethiopia**, one of the few African states never colonized (briefly occupied by Italy 1936-41, then liberated).
  - **Kingdom of Benin** (~1180-1897) produced bronze artwork some of which is in the British Museum still and contested.
- Pre-colonial trade networks moved gold, salt, ivory, and slaves across the Sahara and along the Indian Ocean.

## The development story

- Independence came mostly in the **1960s**.
- The new states inherited weak institutions, arbitrary borders, and immediate Cold War proxy interference. The US and USSR backed dictators on both sides.
- Structural adjustment in the 1980s (IMF and World Bank conditions) cut government spending and forced market opening. Critics argue it crippled state capacity.
- **Africa's GDP per capita actually declined** through the 1970s-90s in many countries.
- Recovery began post-2000. Several countries grew at **~5% annually** between 2000 and 2015.
- COVID and commodity shocks have slowed things since.

## Current dynamics

- **Population boom.** Africa will be roughly **25% of the world population by 2050**. The median age across the continent is under 20.
- **Urbanization.** **Lagos** is already 15+ million. Cairo is similar. Nairobi, Accra, Kinshasa, and Addis Ababa are growing fast.
- **Chinese investment.** The **Belt and Road Initiative** has funded large infrastructure projects across Africa in exchange for resources and access. African leaders welcome the capital; critics warn of debt traps.
- **Mobile technology leapfrog.** Africa skipped landlines for cell phones and skipped traditional banking for mobile money (**M-Pesa** in Kenya pioneered this).
- **Climate frontline.** The Sahel is in chronic drought. Coastal erosion is hitting West Africa. Crop yields are falling in places where farming was already marginal.

## What this means today

- Africa's resources, demographics, and youth make it the most consequential continent of the second half of the 21st century.
- Whether it follows East Asia's growth trajectory or stays trapped in resource dependence depends on governance, infrastructure, climate, and outside investment.
- The geography sets the constraints. It doesn't determine the outcome.

## What to carry away

- Africa is huge and geographically harder to develop than any other continent.
- The Sahara, the lack of natural harbors, the rivers that don't help with trade, and the disease burden were the original constraints.
- Colonial borders added a layer of dysfunction that's still working itself out 60 years after independence.
- Pre-colonial Africa had real empires and trade networks; the "blank slate" narrative was a colonial myth.
- Africa's century is starting. The demographics, the resources, and the urbanization are all moving the same direction.
- In one sentence: Africa is the continent geography made hardest to develop, and it's about to become the most populous and consequential one anyway.
