# Why geography drives geopolitics: the map under the news

## The argument and why it matters

A small group of writers has spent the last two decades making one claim: if you want to understand why nations act the way they do, look at the map first. **Tim Marshall** in *Prisoners of Geography*. **Robert Kaplan** in *The Revenge of Geography*. **John Mearsheimer**, the realist political scientist at Chicago, in *The Tragedy of Great Power Politics*. Their argument is not that geography determines everything. Their argument is that geography sets the default options. Politicians and ideologies and economies change. The mountains, rivers, coasts, and neighbors do not. Once you see this layer, the news stops being a series of unrelated crises and starts looking like a few recurring patterns playing out across continents.

## Russia: paranoia by topography

- Russia sits on the **North European Plain**, a vast flat stretch running from the Pyrenees to the Urals with almost no natural barriers across most of it.
- Russia has been invaded across this plain repeatedly. Napoleon in 1812. The Kaiser's Germany in 1914. Hitler in 1941. Before that the Swedes under Charles XII in 1708. Before that the Mongols in the 1200s, who took Russia by coming from the other direction.
- The Russian response, for 300 years, has been to push the border outward and hold buffer states between the Russian heartland and any potential invader. The Tsars did it. Stalin did it after 1945, locking down Eastern Europe as the Warsaw Pact. Putin has been doing it since 2008 in Georgia, since 2014 in Crimea, and since 2022 in the full Ukraine invasion.
- You do not have to agree with the policy to see the pattern. **Ukraine** matters to Russia because Ukraine sits on the plain Russia keeps getting invaded across, and because losing Ukraine to NATO closes the buffer.
- Russia also has only a handful of warm-water ports, which is why **Sevastopol** in Crimea, **Tartus** in Syria, and access through the Bosphorus matter so much. A country without warm-water ports has its navy frozen in for half the year.

## China: pressure from every direction

- China's geography is harder than it looks on a map. The Himalayas wall off the west. The Gobi desert and the Mongolian steppe to the north. To the east, the Pacific is blocked by the **First Island Chain**: Japan, the Ryukyu Islands, Taiwan, and the Philippines all sit in an arc that closes off easy access to deep ocean.
- This is why **Taiwan** is not just an ideological fight. Taiwan is the gap in the chain. If Taiwan stays in the US orbit, the US can cap Chinese naval power. If Taiwan falls into Chinese control, the Pacific opens up.
- China's other vulnerability is energy imports. Most of China's oil comes by ship through the **Strait of Malacca**. The US Navy patrols Malacca. In a serious confrontation, the US could choke off China's oil supply within weeks. This is called the **Malacca Dilemma** and is the unstated driver of Chinese investment in Central Asian pipelines and the Belt and Road Initiative.
- Internally, China has a deep imbalance: the wealthy coastal provinces and the poorer interior. The Communist Party spends a huge amount of effort holding this together, including Tibet and Xinjiang, both of which sit on the high western edge and are kept in the country by force.

## The United States: the luckiest great power in history

- The US is bordered by two oceans (Atlantic and Pacific) and two weak neighbors (Canada, friendly; Mexico, much smaller).
- No serious land invasion has reached the continental US since the British in 1812. No bombing raid hit the US mainland in either World War.
- The **Mississippi River system** gives the US a natural internal trade highway that drains the most productive farmland on the planet. **New Orleans** was the most valuable city in the world to buy in 1803 because it controlled the river's mouth, which is why Jefferson made the Louisiana Purchase.
- The US has the largest network of navigable waterways of any country, more than the rest of the world combined. Add the two-ocean buffer and the weak neighbors, and the US has the easiest geography of any major power in history. This is most of why it became dominant.
- The US Navy now patrols the global commons (the major sea lanes), which means the US benefits from open global trade more than anyone and has the most ability to disrupt it if it chooses.

## India: peninsular and pressured

- India sits on a peninsula sticking into the Indian Ocean, with the **Himalayas** to the north walling off China.
- The Himalayan barrier is mostly impassable, but the border with **Pakistan** is flat, porous, and was created by the 1947 partition that killed somewhere between a half million and two million people and displaced 15 million. India and Pakistan have fought four wars since.
- India's peninsular shape is a naval advantage. The **Indian Ocean** is the obvious sphere of influence, and the Indian Navy is the dominant local force.
- India's main geographic problem is that the Himalayas catch monsoon rain, which is great for agriculture but also means India is dependent on a single annual weather event for most of its water. Climate change is making this worse.
- India also has a huge population pressing against a finite land area. About **1.4 billion** people in a country roughly one-third the size of the US.

## Africa: the cursed map

- Africa's geography is the hardest of any continent. The **Sahara desert** cuts North Africa off from the rest, and the desert is harder to cross than an ocean. Sub-Saharan Africa was effectively a separate world for most of human history.
- Africa has very few natural deepwater ports. Most of the coast is straight and shallow, which is one reason Africa was hard to colonize from the sea and equally hard to integrate into global trade afterward.
- Most African rivers, despite being huge (Congo, Niger, Zambezi), are not navigable from the ocean because they hit waterfalls and rapids near the coast. The continent had no internal trade highway like the Mississippi or the Rhine.
- The colonial borders drawn by Europeans at the **Berlin Conference of 1884-1885** ignored geography, ethnicity, and existing political structures. Modern African countries inherited shapes that often combine groups that don't get along and split groups that do. Many of Africa's ongoing conflicts trace to this.
- None of this means Africa is doomed. Africa is the youngest continent by median age and has the world's fastest-growing populations. But the geography starts the race uphill.

## Energy and climate: the choke points and the shifting map

- About 20% of global oil moves through the **Strait of Hormuz** between Iran and Oman. This is why the US Navy keeps a fleet there.
- About a quarter of world trade moves through the **Strait of Malacca**.
- Climate change is rewriting the map. The **Arctic** is opening to shipping as sea ice retreats. Russia is positioning to dominate the Northern Sea Route. The Northwest Passage through Canada is becoming viable for parts of the year.
- Water is becoming a flashpoint. The **Nile** (shared by Ethiopia, Sudan, Egypt) is contested over Ethiopia's Grand Renaissance Dam. The **Tigris and Euphrates** are contested between Turkey, Syria, and Iraq. The **Indus** is contested between India and Pakistan.

## The honest qualification

Geography is a constraint, not destiny. South Korea and North Korea have basically the same geography and very different outcomes. Singapore is a tiny island with no resources and is one of the richest countries in the world. Israel is a small desert country with no oil that built a tech economy. Human choices matter.

But the choices are made on top of a map that does not change. **Russia** will keep wanting buffer states even when the leaders change. **China** will keep pressing on the First Island Chain. The US will keep enjoying its two oceans. India will keep watching its monsoon. Geography is the constraint that lasts longest.

## What to carry away

- The map is the slowest-changing layer of the news. Politics changes every election. Geography changes over geologic time.
- Russia wants buffers because it has been invaded across a flat plain for 800 years.
- China wants Taiwan because Taiwan is the gap in the First Island Chain.
- The US won the geography lottery: two oceans, weak neighbors, the Mississippi system.
- India is pinned between the Himalayas, Pakistan, and a single annual monsoon.
- Africa's borders ignore its geography, which is why many conflicts persist.
- Geography is not destiny. It is the floor and the ceiling.
