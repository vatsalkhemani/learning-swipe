# The 10 US states that matter for conversation (and the ones that don't)

## Why 10 specifically and why it matters

There are 50 US states. Americans deeply identify by state, in a way that surprises people from countries where the national identity dominates. But in conversation outside the state itself, only about 10 states come up regularly. The rest exist, vote, have governors and college football teams, and almost never enter a non-political conversation. If you can hold these 10 in your head with one sentence each, you can talk about American politics, business, and culture without getting lost. This is the practical short list.

## The 10 that matter

- **New York.** 19 million people. Home to **New York City**, the financial capital of the world, plus Wall Street, the UN, Broadway, and the dominant media center. The state outside the city is rural and mostly Republican. The city is overwhelmingly Democratic. The state votes blue because the city outweighs everything else. Rarely a swing state.
- **California.** 40 million people, the largest state by population, the biggest economy in the US, and roughly the fifth largest economy in the world if measured as a country. Holds **Silicon Valley** (tech), **Hollywood** (entertainment), and the agricultural Central Valley. Deep blue politically. Has its own ongoing debate about governance, taxes, homelessness, and whether people are leaving for Texas (some are, the data is mixed). When Americans argue about California, they are arguing about whether progressive governance works.
- **Texas.** 30 million people, second largest state by population and by area (Alaska beats it on area). The energy capital of the US (oil, gas, increasingly wind and solar). **Austin** has become the second tech hub after Silicon Valley. Was deep red for decades, now increasingly purple as cities grow and Latino population shifts. Immigration politics run through Texas because most of the US-Mexico border is here.
- **Florida.** 22 million people. The retiree state, the Cuban-American political center (Miami), and a major Latino population overall. Holds **Disney World**, the **Kennedy Space Center**, and a hurricane season every year. Was a swing state for two decades, has shifted reliably red under Ron DeSantis since 2018. Florida politics now drive a lot of national Republican strategy.
- **Illinois.** 12 million people. **Chicago** is the city, the third-largest in the US, the cultural anchor of the Midwest, and the reason Illinois exists in conversation at all. Outside Chicago, Illinois is rural and Republican. The state overall votes blue because Chicago dominates. When people say "Chicago" they usually mean the city, not the state.
- **Massachusetts.** 7 million people. **Boston** is the city. Holds **Harvard**, **MIT**, and a dense cluster of other elite colleges that makes the Boston area arguably the intellectual capital of the country. Deep blue. Often the source of policy templates the rest of the country later adopts (Romneycare became the model for the Affordable Care Act). Small state, outsized influence.
- **Washington.** 8 million people. **Seattle** is the city, **Microsoft** and **Amazon** are both headquartered here, and **Boeing** has deep roots. Blue in the Seattle metro and Puget Sound area, red in eastern Washington and the rural parts. The state's politics, like California's, sit on top of a tech-driven economy.
- **Pennsylvania.** 13 million people. The most important swing state in modern presidential elections. **Philadelphia** in the east, **Pittsburgh** in the west, and a vast rural middle that Pennsylvanians call "Pennsyltucky." Democrats win the cities, Republicans win the rural parts, and the suburbs decide elections. Almost every presidential campaign in the last 20 years has run through Pennsylvania.
- **Georgia.** 11 million people. **Atlanta** is the city, the cultural capital of the Black South, the headquarters of CNN, Coca-Cola, and Delta. Was reliably red until 2020, when sustained voter-registration organizing led by **Stacey Abrams** flipped it to vote for Biden and then both Senate seats. Georgia is now a genuine swing state.
- **Ohio.** 12 million people. The classic **Rust Belt** state: industrial cities (Cleveland, Columbus, Cincinnati) plus heavy manufacturing decline plus rural Appalachian counties in the southeast. Was a swing state through the Obama years. Has moved reliably Republican since 2016 as the white working-class realignment hit. Still studied as the template for Rust Belt politics in Michigan, Wisconsin, and Pennsylvania.

## The honorable mentions

- **Michigan and Wisconsin** behave like Ohio and Pennsylvania. Swing states with auto industry and Rust Belt politics. They come up around elections.
- **New Jersey** is dense, blue, and basically a suburb of NYC and Philly. Comes up in New York conversations more than its own.
- **Virginia** has tilted blue thanks to the DC suburbs in the north (Northern Virginia tech corridor). Used to be a Confederate state, now a moderate blue state.
- **Arizona and Nevada** are new swing states tied to demographic shifts in the Southwest. Phoenix and Las Vegas are growing fast.
- **North Carolina** is the southern swing state, with Research Triangle (Raleigh-Durham-Chapel Hill) driving its educated-economy growth.

## The states that rarely come up

These exist, vote, have full legislatures, and almost never enter a non-political conversation outside their region:

- The Plains states: **North Dakota**, **South Dakota**, **Nebraska**, **Kansas**, **Iowa** (Iowa shows up every four years for the presidential caucuses, then disappears).
- The Mountain West: **Montana**, **Wyoming**, **Idaho**, **Utah** (Utah pokes through occasionally because of the Mormon Church and Salt Lake City).
- The Deep South non-anchors: **Mississippi**, **Alabama**, **West Virginia**, **Arkansas**, **Louisiana** (Louisiana shows up for New Orleans, Hurricane Katrina, Mardi Gras, and energy industry stories).
- The smaller New England states: **Vermont**, **New Hampshire**, **Maine**, **Rhode Island**, **Connecticut** (Connecticut pokes through because of hedge funds in Greenwich).
- The Pacific Northwest minus Washington: **Oregon** (Portland comes up sometimes), **Alaska**, **Hawaii**.

This is not a value judgment. These states matter to the people who live there. They matter in the Senate (each gets two senators, the same as California). They matter for the Electoral College. They just rarely come up in casual non-political conversation outside their own region.

## What to carry away

- 10 states do most of the conversational work: New York, California, Texas, Florida, Illinois, Massachusetts, Washington, Pennsylvania, Georgia, Ohio.
- The pattern: each one has a dominant city or industry that gives it a national identity. New York is Wall Street. California is tech and Hollywood. Texas is energy. Florida is retirees and Cubans. Massachusetts is colleges.
- Swing states are a separate category that overlaps: Pennsylvania, Georgia, Michigan, Wisconsin, Arizona, Nevada, North Carolina. Every four years, presidential campaigns concentrate here.
- If you cannot place a state on the map but you know one sentence about it, you are already ahead of most Americans on their own geography.
