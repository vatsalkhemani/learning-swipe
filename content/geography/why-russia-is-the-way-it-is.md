# Why Russia is the way it is: geography is destiny

## What is Russia's geography and why it matters

Russia is the largest country on Earth by land area, roughly **17 million km²**, spanning about **11 time zones**. Despite that size, it holds only ~**144 million people**, a density around **9 people per km²** (compare India at ~470 or the US at ~37). Most of that population sits in the western fifth of the country; the east is empty and cold. The shape of this land, and the fact that no mountains or seas guard its western edge, has produced a foreign policy of defensive paranoia that goes back three centuries. If you want to understand why Russia acts the way it does, you read the map first.

## The plain that won't stop letting invaders in

- The **North European Plain** runs from France through Germany, Poland, and Belarus, then straight into Russia. No natural barriers. No mountains, no defensible rivers running the right way.
- **Mongols** invaded 1237 and ruled Russian principalities for ~250 years until 1480.
- **Swedes** under Charles XII fought the Great Northern War 1700-1721; Peter the Great beat them at Poltava.
- **Napoleon** invaded in 1812, reached Moscow, found the city burned, retreated in winter. Most of his Grande Armée didn't come home.
- **Germans** invaded in **1914** and again in **1941**. The second one killed about **27 million Soviets**, the worst national loss in modern history.
- The doctrine that emerged from all this: **defense in depth**. Keep buffer states between Russia and any plausible invader. Never fight on Russian soil if you can fight on someone else's.

## Why warm-water ports drove the empire outward

- Russia's coastline is mostly Arctic or Pacific. Long supply lines, frozen part of the year, far from world markets.
- **Crimea** was annexed in **1783** under **Catherine the Great** to put a fleet on the Black Sea. Annexed again in **2014**.
- The Russo-Persian wars pushed south through the Caucasus toward the Persian Gulf.
- The push to **Vladivostok** and Manchuria ended badly with the **Russo-Japanese War of 1904-05**, the first time a European power lost to an Asian one.
- The pursuit of unfrozen, year-round sea access shaped centuries of expansion. The motive was never just glory; it was the map.

## The NATO question and Ukraine

- NATO expanded east between **1999 and 2004**: Poland, Hungary, Czech Republic, then the Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania).
- Moscow reads this as encirclement. The Warsaw Pact had been the formal version of Russia's buffer doctrine; NATO sitting on its border is the inverse.
- **Putin's framing**, made explicit in his June 2022 speech and others before it: the war in Ukraine is about restoring the defensive perimeter.
- Why Ukraine specifically:
  - **Kyiv** predates Moscow as a Slavic capital. The Russian Orthodox Church traces its roots there.
  - Ukraine sits on Europe's most fertile black-earth belt.
  - **Sevastopol** in Crimea is Russia's main warm-water naval base.
  - Ukraine is the buffer between Russia and the rest of Europe.
- Whether you agree with the framing or not, the geographic logic is what's driving the decisions. Without it, Russian behavior looks like sheer aggression. With it, you see a defensive paranoia that's older than the United States.

## The resource curse and climate problem

- Russia holds the **largest natural gas reserves on Earth** and massive oil reserves. Commodities are ~50% of federal budget revenue in most years.
- This produces "Dutch disease" effects: the currency is propped up by resource exports, which makes other industries uncompetitive. Tech, manufacturing, and services all underperform what the talent base should produce.
- Price swings in oil and gas swing the entire economy. The 1998 default and the 2014 ruble crisis both tracked oil prices.
- **Climate** concentrates everything in the west and south. Most arable land sits in a narrow southern band. Siberia is rich in resources but expensive to live in and work.
- Cold winters drove the historical pattern of urbanization in the west, leaving the east a strategic vacuum. China sits across that empty border.

## What this means today

- Russia's strategic behavior is legible as defense, not just offense, once you see the map.
- The dependence on commodities means sanctions hurt slowly but real.
- The population is shrinking and aging. The empty east is a long-term vulnerability that no policy has solved.
- Climate change is one of the few things that might genuinely help Russia, opening Arctic shipping routes and northern agriculture, while hurting almost everyone else.

## What to carry away

- Geography didn't cause every Russian decision, but it set the constraints inside which every Russian decision got made.
- The North European Plain is the single most important fact about Russian foreign policy. Everything traces back to it.
- Warm-water ports, buffer states, NATO encirclement, Ukraine: all of it is the same logic, repeated for three centuries.
- Resource wealth funds the state but also keeps the economy fragile.
- In one sentence: Russia behaves like a country that has been invaded so many times it stopped trusting the map, and started trying to redraw it.
