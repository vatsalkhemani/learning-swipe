# The mental world map: how to actually hold the world in your head

## What a mental world map is and why it matters

A mental world map is the shape of the world you carry in your head before you open Google Maps. Every news story has a setting. Every geopolitical fight, every business expansion, every conversation about where someone is from assumes you can place it. Without that frame, you nod along and lose the meaning. With it, the world stops being a blur of unfamiliar names and starts being a place with patterns. The goal is not memorizing flags. The goal is being able to hear "the situation in **Yemen**" or "**Vietnam** is the new manufacturing hub" and know roughly where, near what, and why that matters.

## The seven continents, quickly

- **North America.** The big three are the **United States** (330M people, third largest country by area), **Canada** (40M, second largest country by area but mostly empty north of the populated southern strip), and **Mexico** (130M, the cultural and economic anchor of Latin America's north). Central America (Guatemala, Honduras, Costa Rica, Panama) hangs off the south. The Caribbean islands sit east.
- **South America.** **Brazil** dominates everything: 215M people, half the continent's land, the only Portuguese-speaking country. Then **Argentina** (Buenos Aires, beef, soccer), **Chile** (long thin Pacific coast, copper, Atacama desert), **Colombia** (50M, coffee, Bogota), and **Venezuela** (oil, collapse, mass migration). **Peru** holds Machu Picchu and the Amazon headwaters.
- **Europe.** Split it three ways. Western (UK, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, the Low Countries, Scandinavia). Central (Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Austria). Eastern (Ukraine, Belarus, the Balkans). The **European Union** is 27 countries with shared rules and mostly a shared currency. The UK left in 2020. Switzerland and Norway never joined. Russia spans Europe and Asia but politically sits apart.
- **Africa.** 54 countries, the number people underestimate most. The **Sahara desert** cuts the continent in two. North Africa (**Egypt**, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya) is Arab-majority, Mediterranean-facing, historically tied to the Middle East. Sub-Saharan Africa is everything below. The four anchors to know: **Nigeria** (220M, most populous African country, Lagos megacity, oil), **Egypt** (110M, the Nile, the Suez Canal), **South Africa** (60M, the most industrialized economy), and **Ethiopia** (120M, the second most populous, never colonized, Addis Ababa hosts the African Union).
- **Asia.** Too big for one frame. Subdivide.
  - **East Asia.** **China** (1.4B), **Japan** (125M), **South Korea** (52M), Taiwan, Mongolia.
  - **Southeast Asia.** The 10 **ASEAN** countries. The big ones for conversation: **Vietnam** (manufacturing rise), **Indonesia** (280M, fourth most populous country in the world, Muslim-majority, Jakarta), **Thailand**, **Philippines**, **Singapore** (tiny, rich, the regional financial hub), **Malaysia**.
  - **South Asia.** **India** (1.4B), **Pakistan** (240M), **Bangladesh** (170M), Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan. India and Pakistan share the Subcontinent and have fought four wars since 1947.
  - **Central Asia.** The five "stans" between Russia and the Middle East: Kazakhstan (the big one, oil, where Borat came from), Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan. Plus Afghanistan, which sits awkwardly between Central and South Asia.
  - **Middle East / West Asia.** The Gulf states (**Saudi Arabia**, **UAE**, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman). The Levant (Israel, Palestine, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Iraq). And the two non-Arab heavyweights, **Iran** (90M, Persian, Shia) and **Turkey** (85M, the bridge between Europe and Asia, secular-Muslim).
- **Oceania.** **Australia** (26M, almost the size of the continental US, mostly empty in the middle), **New Zealand** (5M, two islands east of Australia), and the Pacific Islands (Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Papua New Guinea, the Solomons, plus a scatter of micro-states).
- **Antarctica.** No countries, no permanent population, just research stations and a 1959 treaty that froze territorial claims.

## The 30-40 cities that come up

Most conversations about "the world" are actually about a few dozen cities. Memorize these and the country they sit in:

- **North America.** New York City (US), Los Angeles (US), Chicago (US), San Francisco (US), Washington DC (US), Toronto (Canada), Vancouver (Canada), Mexico City (Mexico).
- **South America.** Sao Paulo (Brazil), Rio de Janeiro (Brazil), Buenos Aires (Argentina), Bogota (Colombia), Lima (Peru), Santiago (Chile).
- **Europe.** London (UK), Paris (France), Berlin (Germany), Madrid (Spain), Rome (Italy), Amsterdam (Netherlands), Brussels (Belgium, also EU capital), Zurich (Switzerland), Vienna (Austria), Moscow (Russia), Istanbul (Turkey, the only city on two continents).
- **Asia.** Tokyo (Japan), Beijing (China, capital), Shanghai (China, financial), Hong Kong (China), Seoul (South Korea), Mumbai (India, financial), Delhi (India, capital), Bangalore (India, tech), Bangkok (Thailand), Singapore (Singapore), Jakarta (Indonesia), Ho Chi Minh City (Vietnam), Dubai (UAE), Tehran (Iran).
- **Middle East / Africa.** Cairo (Egypt), Lagos (Nigeria), Johannesburg (South Africa), Nairobi (Kenya), Tel Aviv (Israel), Riyadh (Saudi Arabia).
- **Oceania.** Sydney (Australia), Melbourne (Australia), Auckland (New Zealand).

That is roughly 40 cities. You can talk to anyone, almost anywhere, with this list.

## Time zones, briefly

- The world is sliced into 24 time zones, each one hour apart, all measured from **UTC** (Coordinated Universal Time), which used to be called GMT and is set at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London.
- US Eastern is UTC-5, US Pacific is UTC-8, India is UTC+5:30 (yes, half-hour offset, weird but historical), China is UTC+8 for the entire country despite being five time zones wide (political choice by Mao to use Beijing time everywhere), Tokyo is UTC+9, Sydney is UTC+10 or +11 depending on daylight saving.
- The practical rule: if it's morning in the US, it's evening in Asia. If it's noon in London, it's morning in New York and evening in Mumbai. That alone covers most scheduling intuitions.

## What to carry away

- You do not need to memorize 195 countries. You need maybe 40 names, 40 cities, and the seven-continent frame.
- The world makes more sense once you can hang every news story on a piece of land. "The conflict in Sudan" stops being abstract once you know Sudan sits south of Egypt, north of South Sudan, on the Red Sea.
- Geography is a memory game with a payoff that compounds: the more you know, the more new information sticks, because every new fact attaches to a place you already have in your head.
- The honest version: nobody knows every flag. Knowing the 30-40 most-mentioned places saves you in every cocktail party and every news cycle. Build that core first. The rest fills in over years.
