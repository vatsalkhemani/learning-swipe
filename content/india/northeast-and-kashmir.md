# The Northeast and Kashmir: India's most complicated borders

## What they are and why they matter

Kashmir and India's Northeast are the two regions where the standard story of India breaks down. Both have populations that don't fit the dominant cultural template. Both have been sites of ongoing armed conflict for decades. Both are heavily militarized. Both involve unresolved territorial disputes with neighbors (Pakistan and China for Kashmir, China for Arunachal). Decisions made about these regions shape India's foreign policy, its defense budget, its constitutional debates, and increasingly its domestic politics. Understanding India without these two is understanding a country whose actual borders you can't see.

## Kashmir: how the dispute started

- In **August 1947**, when British India was partitioned, the **princely states** (semi-autonomous kingdoms under British paramountcy, ~565 of them) had to choose between joining India, joining Pakistan, or trying for independence.
- **Jammu & Kashmir** was a strange case. Its ruler, **Maharaja Hari Singh**, was Hindu. His population was roughly 77% Muslim. Hari Singh stalled, wanting independence.
- **October 1947.** Tribal raiders from Pakistan's North-West Frontier (with Pakistani military backing) invaded Kashmir, heading for Srinagar. Hari Singh panicked, signed the **Instrument of Accession to India**, and India airlifted troops.
- The **first Indo-Pak war (1947-48)** ended with a **UN-brokered ceasefire** in January 1949. The ceasefire line became the **Line of Control (LoC)**. Neither side accepted it as a permanent border.

## How Kashmir is divided

- **India holds about 55%.** Jammu & Kashmir Union Territory and Ladakh Union Territory.
- **Pakistan holds about 30%.** "Azad Kashmir" (Pakistan-administered) and Gilgit-Baltistan (administered separately).
- **China holds about 15%.** **Aksai Chin**, taken in the **1962 Sino-Indian War**. Mostly uninhabited high-altitude desert but strategically important for China's Tibet-Xinjiang road.
- Three nuclear powers, all claiming overlapping territory. There is no other border like this on earth.

## Article 370 and what it did

- The Indian Constitution granted J&K **special autonomy** under **Article 370 (1949)**. The state had its own constitution, its own flag, and residency restrictions (only "permanent residents" could buy property).
- The article was meant to be temporary. It lasted 70 years.
- Critics argued it kept J&K underdeveloped, distorted, and isolated from the rest of India. Supporters argued it was the deal that kept Kashmir in India in the first place.

## The militancy and the lockdown

- **1989-90.** A combination of rigged elections, Pakistani support for jihadi groups, and indigenous Kashmiri resentment exploded into armed insurgency. Pakistan-trained militants crossed the LoC. Local groups (JKLF originally, then more Islamist factions) emerged.
- The Indian state responded with massive military deployment. **AFSPA (Armed Forces Special Powers Act)** gave soldiers broad immunity from prosecution.
- **Roughly 70,000 people** have been killed in Kashmir since 1989 by various estimates, including militants, security forces, and civilians.
- **Around 500,000 Kashmiri Pandits** (the Hindu minority of the Valley) were displaced in the early 1990s after targeted killings. Most have not returned.
- **August 2019.** The Modi government **abrogated Article 370**, stripped J&K of statehood, and split it into two Union Territories (J&K and Ladakh). The move was preceded by a security lockdown, mass detentions of political leaders, and a communications blackout that lasted months in some areas.
- The constitutionality of the move is still being contested in the Supreme Court. The political legitimacy is contested on the ground.

## The Northeast: what it is

- **Eight states.** The "**Seven Sisters**" (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura) plus **Sikkim**, often grouped together.
- **~45 million people.** Around 3.7% of India's population.
- **Ethnically distinct.** Many groups are **Tibeto-Burman** or **Austroasiatic**, culturally and linguistically separate from the Indo-Aryan and Dravidian populations that dominate mainland India.
- **~250+ recognized tribes.** Each with its own language, customs, sometimes its own micro-territory.
- **Religion is mixed.** Christian-majority in several states: **Nagaland (~88%)**, **Mizoram (~87%)**, **Meghalaya (~74%)**. Hindu-majority in Assam, Tripura, Manipur. Buddhist in Sikkim and Arunachal. Indigenous animist traditions still alive in many areas.
- **The Siliguri Corridor.** The narrow strip of Indian territory between Bhutan and Bangladesh, about **22 km wide at its narrowest**, that physically connects the Northeast to the rest of India. Nicknamed the "**Chicken's Neck**." A strategic obsession.

## The Northeast conflicts

- **Nagaland.** The **Naga insurgency** is one of the world's longest-running. Began in 1947 when Naga leaders declared independence the day before India did. The **National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN)** has been in on-and-off negotiations with the Indian government since 1997. Still unresolved.
- **Manipur.** Multiple insurgent groups historically. **2023 ethnic violence** between the **Meitei** (Hindu majority, valley dwellers) and **Kuki** (Christian tribal, hill dwellers) communities killed around **200 people**, displaced tens of thousands, destroyed hundreds of churches and temples. Houses on fire for months.
- **Assam.** The **ULFA (United Liberation Front of Asom)** insurgency demanded a sovereign Assam. Largely defunct now but the underlying demographic anxiety remains. The **NRC (National Register of Citizens) controversy** in 2019 left ~1.9 million people in Assam off the official citizen list, mostly Bengali Muslims, in a process meant to identify "illegal Bangladeshi immigrants."
- **Mizoram.** Largely peaceful now after a 1986 peace accord ended a 20-year insurgency.
- **Arunachal Pradesh.** Not internally restive but **claimed by China as "South Tibet."** A flashpoint in India-China relations. Chinese troops have intruded multiple times. **Tawang**, in western Arunachal, is the spiritual home of a major Tibetan Buddhist monastery and is what China most wants.
- **Migration tensions.** Across the Northeast, debates about "outsiders" (mainland Indians, Bangladeshi immigrants) shape local politics in ways the rest of India often misreads.

## The development push

- The Modi government's "**Act East**" policy emphasized Northeast development and connectivity. Roads, rail, airports. The **Bogibeel Bridge** (Assam, 2018) and the **Sela Tunnel** (Arunachal, 2024) are flagship infrastructure projects.
- Tourism opened up. Sikkim, Meghalaya, and Arunachal now have growing tourism. Roads improved dramatically.
- Connectivity to **Myanmar** (and through Myanmar to Southeast Asia) is the strategic vision: turn the Northeast from India's dead end into India's gateway east. Whether that vision survives Myanmar's civil war is unclear.

## What people get wrong

- These regions are not just "Indian states with extra problems." They have populations whose connection to the national story has always been ambiguous and contested.
- Kashmir is not only a Hindu-Muslim issue. There are Buddhist Ladakhis, Shia Muslims, Pandits, Sufi traditions, and political identities that don't fit either Indian or Pakistani nationalism cleanly.
- The Northeast is not one region. Nagaland and Assam have almost nothing in common other than being adjacent. Generalizing is the standard mistake.

## What to carry away

- Kashmir involves three nuclear powers, an unresolved partition, an ongoing insurgency, and a constitutional move (2019) whose legitimacy is still being fought.
- The Northeast is eight states with deep ethnic complexity, multiple insurgencies past and present, and a thin physical link to the rest of India.
- Both regions disproportionately shape India's foreign policy, defense budget, and constitutional debates.
- The standard Indian story (Hindu-Muslim, Indo-Aryan-Dravidian, mainland) does not capture either of them.
