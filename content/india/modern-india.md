# Modern India: British Raj to today, the clean 5-minute version

## What modern India is and why it matters

Modern India is the story of a country going from a stripped colony to the world's fifth largest economy in three generations. The arc is unusual. Most post-colonial nations either stayed poor or traded democracy for fast growth. India tried to keep both, paid a real cost in speed for decades, and is now in the part of the curve where things compound. The country holds 17% of humanity inside a single democratic system, in 22 official languages, with no civil war since independence. Understanding this arc is how you understand what India is betting on now.

## The British Raj (1858-1947)

- The **British Crown** took direct control after the 1857 uprising, ending the East India Company's rule. The setup that followed lasted 89 years.
- Real things were built. **Railways** (one of the largest networks in the world by 1900), **universities** (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras founded 1857), the **Indian Civil Service**, English-language administration, postal system, telegraph.
- The economy underneath was extractive. India's share of world GDP fell from roughly 24% in 1700 to about 4% by 1947. Cotton exports flowed one way, finished textiles the other.
- **Famines** were the brutal counterweight. The **Bengal famine of 1943** killed an estimated 2-3 million while wartime grain was diverted to British troops. **Winston Churchill** refused to redirect supplies. The wound is still live in Indian historical memory.
- The independence movement built itself across decades. **Mohandas Gandhi** returned from South Africa in 1915 and converted the Congress into a mass movement using satyagraha, non-violent civil disobedience. Salt March 1930. Quit India 1942.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** ran the Congress as a Cambridge-educated socialist, the Westernized inheritor.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** offered the alternative path. He raised the Indian National Army with Japanese support, believed independence required force, died in a plane crash in 1945. He remains the icon for Indians who think Gandhi was too soft.

## 1947 Partition and the Nehru era

- Independence came on 15 August 1947. So did **Partition**. British lawyer **Cyril Radcliffe**, who had never been to India, was given five weeks to draw the line splitting India and Pakistan.
- The result: 10-15 million people displaced, roughly 1 million dead in communal violence, the largest mass migration in human history. Punjab and Bengal were torn down the middle. Lahore, an Indian city for centuries, became Pakistan. Karachi, full of Hindus, emptied out.
- **Nehru** became Prime Minister and ran the country for 17 years until his death in 1964. He built four things that stuck.
  - A **secular constitution** adopted 1950, drafted under **B R Ambedkar**, a Dalit jurist who later converted to Buddhism in protest of caste.
  - A **socialist-leaning planned economy** with Soviet-style Five Year Plans and a heavy public sector.
  - The **Non-Aligned Movement**, refusing to pick US or USSR camps.
  - The **IITs and AIIMS**, science and engineering institutions Nehru called "the temples of modern India," along with the big dams.
- Nehru lost the 1962 war to **China** in the Himalayas. The defeat broke his health and his foreign policy worldview at once.

## Indira and Rajiv (1966-1991)

- **Indira Gandhi**, Nehru's daughter, became Prime Minister in 1966. Tough, centralizing, unsentimental. She nationalized banks in 1969, broke Pakistan in the 1971 Bangladesh war (Indian Army won decisively in 13 days), and tested India's first nuclear device in 1974.
- The **Emergency (1975-77)** was her dark chapter. After a court found her guilty of election fraud, she suspended civil liberties, jailed opposition leaders, censored the press, and ran a forced sterilization program under her son Sanjay. She lost the 1977 election, the first time Congress had lost power. She came back in 1980.
- **Operation Blue Star (1984)** sent the Indian Army into the Golden Temple in Amritsar to flush out Sikh militants. Months later her own Sikh bodyguards assassinated her. The aftermath included anti-Sikh pogroms in Delhi that killed around 3,000 people.
- **Rajiv Gandhi**, her son, took over. Less ideological, more technocratic. Pushed computerization and telecom in the late 1980s, the first stirrings of modernization. Lost the 1989 election. Was assassinated by a Tamil Tiger suicide bomber in 1991 while campaigning.

## 1991 liberalization and the new India

- A balance-of-payments crisis in 1991 left India with three weeks of foreign exchange. The country had to airlift gold reserves to London.
- Prime Minister **P V Narasimha Rao** and Finance Minister **Manmohan Singh** used the crisis to push through sweeping liberalization in July 1991. Industrial delicensing, currency devaluation, FDI opened up, tariffs cut. The License Raj ended.
- Growth accelerated from the old "Hindu rate" of 3.5% to 6-7% sustained.
- **Atal Bihari Vajpayee** of the **BJP** became Prime Minister in 1998, the first non-Congress PM to complete a full term. India conducted nuclear tests at **Pokhran** in May 1998, weathered sanctions, opened the highways program.
- **Manmohan Singh** himself became Prime Minister in 2004 and served 10 years. UPA government, coalition with the left. Right to Information Act, MGNREGA rural jobs program, India-US civil nuclear deal in 2008.

## The Modi era (2014-now)

- **Narendra Modi** and the BJP won an outright parliamentary majority in 2014, the first single-party majority since 1984.
- Big moves came fast and were polarizing. **Demonetization** in November 2016 invalidated 86% of cash overnight to fight black money. **GST** in 2017 unified the tax code. **Article 370 abrogation** in 2019 stripped Jammu and Kashmir of its special status. **COVID** in 2020-21 hit hard, killed at least 4.7 million by excess-mortality estimates, then the vaccine rollout reached over a billion doses.
- India became the **5th largest economy** in 2022, passing the UK. Goldman Sachs projects 3rd place by 2027.
- India hosted **G20** in 2023, used it as a foreign policy showcase, launched the African Union into permanent G20 membership.

## The pattern

- Democracy held through everything, including an Emergency. No country with this much diversity and this little money has kept a democracy this long.
- Growth was slow for decades, then sudden. Liberalization in 1991 was the inflection point. The compounding only became visible 20 years later.
- The fault lines are real. Hindu-Muslim tensions, caste, North-South, urban-rural. They have not been resolved. They have been managed inside a constitutional system that mostly holds.
- In one sentence: modern India is democracy plus diversity plus slow-then-sudden growth, and the next decade is when the curve gets steep.
