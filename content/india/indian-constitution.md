# The Indian Constitution: the longest written constitution and how Ambedkar built it

## What it is and why it matters

The Indian Constitution, adopted on **January 26, 1950**, is the world's longest written national constitution. It is around **146,385 words** with **25 parts**, **12 schedules**, and roughly **450 articles** after amendments (originally 395). It set up a sovereign secular democratic republic with universal adult suffrage from day one, a federal structure, an independent judiciary, and an enforceable bill of rights. The document is closer to a national operating manual than a short philosophical statement. It is also the central reference point for every major political fight in modern India. Anyone who wants to follow Indian politics has to know what it says, what it doesn't, and what the Supreme Court has decided it really means.

## How it was built

- The **Constituent Assembly** was elected in 1946 with 389 members, later 299 after partition. It met for nearly three years (December 1946 to November 1949) and held 165 days of session.
- The **Drafting Committee** had seven members. It was chaired by **B.R. Ambedkar (1891-1956)**, who became the face of the document.
- Ambedkar's biography matters. Born **Dalit** (then called "untouchable") in a community ritually excluded from temples, wells, and basic public life. PhD from **Columbia University** under John Dewey, doctorate from the **London School of Economics**, called to the bar at Gray's Inn. India's **first law minister**. The architect of India's reservation system (affirmative action) for Scheduled Castes and Tribes. Converted to **Buddhism in 1956** with several hundred thousand followers in a single Nagpur ceremony as a final rejection of caste Hinduism.
- The text drew on multiple sources: the British parliamentary system, the US Bill of Rights, the Irish Directive Principles, the Canadian federal model, the Australian concurrent list, and Weimar Germany's emergency provisions. The assembly was openly comparative.

## The structure

- **The Preamble.** Declares India a "**Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**." "Socialist" and "Secular" were added later by the **42nd Amendment** in 1976 during Indira Gandhi's Emergency. The original 1950 Preamble did not include those words.
- **Part III - Fundamental Rights.** Judicially enforceable. Equality before law (Article 14), freedom of speech and assembly (Article 19), freedom of religion (Article 25), right against exploitation (Article 23), right to constitutional remedies (Article 32, which Ambedkar called "the heart and soul" of the constitution).
- **Part IV - Directive Principles of State Policy.** Aspirational, not enforceable. Goals like adequate livelihood, equal pay, free legal aid, universal education, cottage industries, environmental protection. The constitution's "wish list" for what governments should aim for.
- **Part IVA - Fundamental Duties.** Added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976. Citizen duties to respect the constitution, the flag, national unity, etc. Largely symbolic.
- **Separation of powers.** Executive (President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers), Legislature (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha), Judiciary (Supreme Court, High Courts, lower courts).
- **Federalism.** Union List, State List, Concurrent List. Strong central tilt compared to the US system. The Union can override states on Concurrent List subjects.
- **Reservations.** Special provisions for Scheduled Castes (~15% of population), Scheduled Tribes (~8.6%), and Other Backward Classes (~40%, added by **Mandal Commission** implementation in 1990). Quotas in legislatures, government jobs, and public education.
- **Language.** Hindi declared official language. English allowed to continue indefinitely as an "associate official language." **22 languages** in the Eighth Schedule have official recognition.

## What was radical about it

- **Universal adult suffrage from day one.** Every Indian adult got the vote in **1950**, regardless of literacy, caste, gender, or property. Compare: the United States did not have full universal suffrage until the **1965 Voting Rights Act** (and earlier the 19th Amendment in 1920 for women). Switzerland did not give women the vote until 1971. India did it at independence, when literacy was around 18%. The decision was made deliberately, against advice to gate suffrage on education.
- **Outlawing untouchability (Article 17).** Made discrimination on the basis of caste a punishable offense.
- **Affirmative action built in.** Reservations for SC/ST were constitutionally entrenched, not left to legislation.
- **The bill of rights was enforceable.** Article 32 gave any citizen the right to approach the Supreme Court directly when fundamental rights are violated. Most constitutional systems leave this much harder.

## The major amendments

- **1st Amendment (1951).** Added "reasonable restrictions" on free speech. Restricted property rights to enable land reform.
- **42nd Amendment (1976).** Passed during the Emergency. Added "Socialist" and "Secular" to the Preamble. Added Fundamental Duties. Curtailed judicial review. Called the "mini-constitution" because of how much it changed.
- **44th Amendment (1978).** Janata government reversed many Emergency-era changes after Indira lost the 1977 election. Made it harder to declare future Emergencies.
- **73rd and 74th Amendments (1992-93).** Constitutionalized **panchayati raj** (village governance) and **municipal governance**. Local democracy got constitutional standing. Mandated reservations for women in local bodies.
- **86th Amendment (2002).** Made education a fundamental right for children aged 6-14.
- **101st Amendment (2017).** Enabled the **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**, India's biggest tax reform.
- **Total amendments to date: ~106.** More than the US (27) but fewer than some commonly cited examples.

## The Basic Structure doctrine

- The **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)** is the most important constitutional decision in Indian history. A 13-judge bench split 7-6 to rule that **Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot alter its "basic structure."**
- What is "basic structure"? Democracy, secularism, federalism, judicial review, rule of law, separation of powers, free and fair elections. Never exhaustively listed. Decided case by case.
- The doctrine is what stopped the 42nd Amendment's worst overreaches and remains the central check on any future government trying to fundamentally remake the constitutional order.
- No major constitutional system globally has anything quite like it. India is the laboratory.

## The hot-button articles

- **Article 370.** Gave Jammu & Kashmir special autonomy from 1949 onward (separate constitution, flag, residency rules). **Abrogated in August 2019** by the Modi government. J&K split into two Union Territories.
- **Article 35A.** Related provision restricting property rights in J&K to "permanent residents." Removed alongside 370.
- **Article 25-28.** Religious freedom provisions. Central to ongoing fights over the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA, 2019), the Uniform Civil Code debate, and Hindu nationalist proposals.
- **Articles 14, 15, 16.** Equality and non-discrimination. The constitutional basis for the long-running reservation debates.

## What to carry away

- The Indian Constitution is long because it had to be: a vast country, many languages, deep inequalities, and no shared constitutional tradition to lean on.
- It is the most ambitious legal document any postcolonial society has produced. Universal suffrage and an enforceable bill of rights from day one is not a small thing.
- Ambedkar's role is real. He did not draft every line, but he chaired the committee, defended every controversial choice on the floor, and made the document politically possible.
- Every major Indian political fight today is, at some level, a fight about how to read this document.
