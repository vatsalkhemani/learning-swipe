# India's tech industry rise: from IT services to product giants

## What it is and why it matters

In 1991 India barely had a private tech industry. By 2024 it was the world's third-largest startup ecosystem, had built the world's most advanced public digital infrastructure, and supplied CEOs to **Microsoft**, **Google**, **Adobe**, **IBM**, and many others. The rise happened in three waves: IT services in the 1990s, captive global delivery centers in the 2000s, and consumer + product startups in the 2010s. Layered on top is India's public tech stack (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker) which most countries don't have and Indian banks now build on for free. Understanding modern India means understanding this transformation, because tech is the most globally visible thing the country produces.

## How IT services exploded

- **The 1991 reforms** opened up the economy. Phones, foreign exchange, and travel suddenly became possible. Software exports were exempted from many controls.
- **The Y2K bug (1999-2000)** was the accelerant. Every major company in the world needed to fix decades of legacy code before midnight on January 1, 2000. India had thousands of English-speaking engineers willing to work at one-fifth Western wages. The order book exploded.
- **The Big Three.**
  - **TCS (Tata Consultancy Services).** Founded 1968 as part of the Tata group. Listed 2004. Now India's largest IT services firm with ~$30B revenue and ~600,000+ employees.
  - **Infosys.** Founded **1981** by **Narayana Murthy** and six co-founders with **~$250 borrowed from Murthy's wife Sudha**. IPO in 1993 was undersubscribed; the first ADR listing on NASDAQ in 1999 made the founders rich. Market cap now ~$80B+, revenue $20B+.
  - **Wipro.** **Azim Premji** inherited his father's vegetable oil business in 1966 (he was 21) and slowly turned it into a software giant. Premji is now one of India's largest philanthropists, with ~$20B+ given to education.
- **The supporting cast.** **HCL Technologies**, **Tech Mahindra**, **Mphasis**, **Cognizant** (NASDAQ-listed, founded in Chennai but with US headquarters).
- **Scale by 2010.** Indian IT services was a $100B+ industry employing roughly **4 million people**, contributing ~7-8% of GDP. The middle class that built itself in Bangalore, Hyderabad, Chennai, and Pune is the political demographic that shaped the next decade.

## Bangalore as Silicon Valley of India

- **Bangalore** (now Bengaluru) became the headquarters of Indian tech because of three accidents: (1) it had **public sector tech presence** from the 1960s (HAL, ISRO, BEL), (2) the weather was tolerable year-round, (3) **Texas Instruments** opened the first major foreign R&D center there in **1985**.
- The **global captive centers (GCCs)** followed. **Microsoft**, **Google**, **Amazon**, **Goldman Sachs**, **JP Morgan**, **Oracle**, **SAP**, **Cisco**, and hundreds of others now have major Bangalore operations. Many of their R&D and engineering centers are larger than their US offices.
- Hyderabad and Pune became secondary hubs. Chennai is strong in services and increasingly in product.

## The product startup wave

- For decades India had services, not products. That started shifting in the 2010s.
- **Flipkart.** Founded **2007** by **Sachin Bansal** and **Binny Bansal** (no relation). The Amazon-of-India play. Sold to **Walmart in 2018 for $16 billion**, India's largest startup exit ever.
- **Ola.** Founded 2010. Ride-hailing. Has struggled since against Uber India.
- **Paytm.** Founded 2010 by **Vijay Shekhar Sharma**. Digital payments, then super-app ambitions. **Controversial IPO in November 2021** at a $20B valuation; stock collapsed ~75% within a year. The IPO became a cautionary tale.
- **Zomato.** Founded 2008. Food delivery + restaurant discovery. IPO 2021.
- **Swiggy.** Founded 2014. Food delivery. IPO 2024.
- **BYJU'S.** Edtech founded 2011 by **Byju Raveendran**. Peaked at **$22B valuation** in 2022, then collapsed amid governance and accounting issues 2023-24. The cautionary tale alongside Paytm.
- **Nykaa.** Founded 2012 by **Falguni Nayar** (former investment banker, mid-40s when she started). Beauty e-commerce. IPO 2021.
- **Lenskart.** Eyewear retail and manufacturing. **$5B+ valuation**, profitable, planned IPO.
- **Razorpay.** Payments infrastructure. Among the most valuable Indian B2B SaaS companies.
- **Zerodha.** The bootstrapped outlier. Founded 2010 by **Nithin Kamath** and **Nikhil Kamath**. **No VC money ever taken.** Profitable from year three. Now ~$2B revenue, India's largest stockbroker by active users. The Kamath brothers are cult figures for proving you can build large without VC.

## The numbers

- **Indian unicorns: ~110+** as of 2024. Was around 10 in 2018.
- India is the **third-largest startup ecosystem** globally after the US and China.
- Most founders are still IIT, IIM, or BITS Pilani educated, but **first-generation entrepreneurship** is rising fast.
- Funding boom in 2021 was followed by a brutal **"funding winter" in 2022-24** that wiped out marginal companies and forced layoffs across the sector.

## The India Stack

- The most distinctive thing India built isn't a startup. It is public digital infrastructure that any private company can use for free.
- **Aadhaar.** A 12-digit unique biometric ID. ~**1.4 billion people enrolled**. The world's largest biometric ID system. Allows instant electronic identity verification for any service. Controversial on privacy grounds; mostly accepted as inevitable now.
- **UPI (Unified Payments Interface).** Launched **2016** by the **NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India)**. A single rails system that lets any bank app talk to any other bank app and any merchant. **~14 billion transactions per month** by 2024 (more than Visa and Mastercard globally combined for retail volume). Made digital payments universal across India in five years.
- **DigiLocker, e-KYC, FASTag**, the **ONDC** (Open Network for Digital Commerce). All sitting on top of Aadhaar + UPI.
- The "**India model**" of public digital infrastructure is now being studied and exported. Brazil's Pix, the World Bank's digital ID programs, and several African systems are explicitly modeled on it.

## The Indian-origin tech CEO phenomenon

- **Satya Nadella** (CEO Microsoft, since 2014). Hyderabad, Manipal Institute of Technology.
- **Sundar Pichai** (CEO Alphabet/Google, since 2015). Chennai, IIT Kharagpur.
- **Shantanu Narayen** (CEO Adobe, since 2007). Hyderabad, Osmania University.
- **Arvind Krishna** (CEO IBM, since 2020). Andhra Pradesh, IIT Kanpur.
- **Leena Nair** (CEO Chanel, since 2022). Maharashtra, XLRI Jamshedpur.
- **Indra Nooyi** (former CEO PepsiCo, 2006-2018). Chennai, IIM Calcutta.
- Common pattern: top-tier Indian engineering or management education, US graduate degree, slow climb up a Fortune 500 ladder. Not founders. Operators.

## The challenges ahead

- **AI talent leaving.** Most of the global AI executive class with Indian origins is in California, not India. Building frontier AI inside India has been hard.
- **The funding winter** of 2022-24 hit unicorns hard. Valuations cut, layoffs across the sector, IPOs delayed or canceled.
- **Regulatory uncertainty** around fintech, crypto, gaming, and data privacy keeps changing the rules.
- **Education quality at scale.** The IITs and IIMs are excellent. The next 5,000 colleges are not. Producing world-class talent in millions, not thousands, is the unsolved problem.
- The next decade is about whether India can build companies on the scale of US and Chinese tech giants, not just supply CEOs to other people's companies.

## What to carry away

- India went from no real private tech industry in 1991 to the world's third-largest startup ecosystem in about 30 years.
- IT services made the middle class. Captive centers made the talent base. Product startups are the work in progress.
- The India Stack (Aadhaar + UPI + others) is the most underrated thing India built. No other country has anything quite like it.
- Indian-origin executives now run a meaningful chunk of global tech. Whether India can build the equivalent companies at home is the open question of the next decade.
