# The Indian political system: parliament, federalism, and how the parties actually differ

## What the Indian political system is and why it matters

India runs a parliamentary democracy modeled on Westminster, with a federal structure that the constitution itself tilts toward the Center. The system has held an election every five years since 1951, transferred power peacefully many times, and absorbed shocks the textbook said should break it. The party system shifted in 2014 from coalition politics to single-party dominance for the first time in 30 years. Understanding the machinery, who actually has power, and what the parties actually believe is the difference between reading Indian news as noise and reading it as a story.

## How the machinery works

- The **Constitution of 1950** is the world's longest written constitution. **B R Ambedkar** chaired the drafting committee. Borrows from Britain (parliamentary form), the US (fundamental rights), Ireland (directive principles), Canada (federalism with strong Center).
- Parliament has two houses.
  - The **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) is the lower house. 543 seats, directly elected by the public every 5 years, first-past-the-post. This is where power lives. Whoever leads the majority here becomes Prime Minister.
  - The **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) is the upper house. 245 seats, indirectly elected by state legislatures, staggered 6-year terms. Closer to the US Senate in design but weaker in practice. It can delay money bills but not block them.
- The **Prime Minister** is the real executive. Heads the cabinet, sets policy, runs the country. The PM is whoever can command a majority in the Lok Sabha.
- The **President** is the head of state, mostly ceremonial. Elected by an electoral college of MPs and state legislators. Signs bills, can return them once. Real power only kicks in if no party has a clear majority and someone has to be asked to form a government. **Droupadi Murmu**, a tribal woman from Odisha, has held the office since 2022.
- The **Supreme Court** is genuinely powerful. Can strike down constitutional amendments under the "basic structure" doctrine it invented in the 1973 **Kesavananda Bharati** case. That doctrine is what stops a parliamentary majority from rewriting the constitution at will.

## Federalism in name, Center-heavy in practice

- India has **28 states** and **8 Union Territories**. States have their own elected legislatures and Chief Ministers.
- Powers are divided into three lists in the constitution.
  - **Union List** (defense, foreign affairs, currency, atomic energy): Center only.
  - **State List** (police, public health, agriculture): states only.
  - **Concurrent List** (education, marriage, forests): both, but Union law wins if there's a conflict.
- **Residuary powers** (anything not listed) sit with the Center. The US gives those to the states. India does the opposite. This is the structural tilt.
- The Center controls the money. **GST**, introduced 2017, pooled most indirect tax revenue into a Council the Center dominates. State Chief Ministers complain about it constantly. It hasn't been reversed.
- **Governors** are appointed by the Center to each state. They've become a flashpoint. Non-BJP state governments accuse Center-appointed governors of stalling bills and meddling in coalition formation.

## The party landscape

- **Indian National Congress** (founded 1885) was the party of independence and ran India for most of the first 40 years. Built modern India in its image: secular, socialist-leaning, English-speaking elite. **Sonia Gandhi**, Italian-born widow of Rajiv, ran the party from 1998 to 2017. Her son **Rahul Gandhi** is the public face but **Mallikarjun Kharge**, a Dalit veteran from Karnataka, took the formal president role in 2022. The party has been out of national power since 2014. Strong in Karnataka, Kerala (off-and-on), parts of the Northeast. Diminished but not gone.
- **Bharatiya Janata Party** (founded 1980 from the older Jana Sangh) is the Hindu nationalist counter-pole. The **RSS** (Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh), founded 1925, is the organizational and ideological parent. RSS workers do the ground operation that wins BJP elections. **Narendra Modi** has led the party at the national level since 2014, with **Amit Shah** as the strategic operator. The BJP won outright Lok Sabha majorities in 2014 and 2019, leads the NDA coalition government from 2024 onwards.
- **Regional parties** matter more than outsiders realize. Some that run states right now:
  - **DMK** in Tamil Nadu (Dravidian, anti-Hindi, strongly federalist, led by M K Stalin).
  - **TMC** (Trinamool Congress) in West Bengal (founded by **Mamata Banerjee**, a Congress breakaway, very personal politics).
  - **AAP** (Aam Aadmi Party), born from the 2011 anti-corruption Anna Hazare movement, runs Delhi and Punjab. **Arvind Kejriwal** as the founder figure.
  - **BRS** (Bharat Rashtra Samithi) in Telangana, led by K Chandrashekar Rao.
  - **JDU** in Bihar, led by **Nitish Kumar**, the great coalition-switcher of Indian politics.

## What separates the parties

- **Secular vs Hindutva.** Congress and the regional parties broadly defend a secular state model. The BJP and its allies push **Hindutva**, the position that India is civilizationally Hindu and the polity should reflect that. Concrete touchpoints: Ram Mandir at Ayodhya (BJP delivered 2024), Article 370 abrogation, Uniform Civil Code debates.
- **Centralized vs federal.** BJP under Modi has pulled power toward the Center. Regional parties and Congress push back, especially in the South where development metrics are higher and resentment about Hindi imposition runs hot.
- **Welfare vs pro-business.** This one is the messiest. The BJP runs the most ambitious cash-transfer and welfare schemes in Indian history (PM Kisan, Ujjwala gas connections, Awas housing) while also being seen as pro-business. Congress historically claimed the welfare mantle. The lines have blurred.
- **Realignments happen.** Nitish Kumar of JDU has switched between BJP-led and Congress-led coalitions multiple times. The Shiv Sena split in two. Parties are vehicles for power coalitions, not fixed ideological blocs.

## Why the coalition era ended in 2014

- From 1989 to 2014, no single party won a Lok Sabha majority. Coalitions ran the show. The era of constant horse-trading, minority governments, regional kingmakers.
- The BJP's **2014 majority** under Modi was the first single-party majority since Rajiv Gandhi's 1984 Congress win after his mother's assassination. They repeated it in 2019, then lost the majority in 2024 and now run a coalition again. The 2014-19 period was the anomaly inside a longer pattern of coalition politics.

## What to carry away

- Power lives in the Lok Sabha and the Prime Minister. Everything else is constraint or ceremony.
- Federalism is real but the Center is structurally dominant. The Center owns the residuary and the money.
- The party axis is secular-vs-Hindutva and centralized-vs-federal, but loyalties are pragmatic.
- The Supreme Court's basic structure doctrine is the actual check on majoritarian power, more than any opposition party.
- In one sentence: India is a Westminster parliament with a Center-heavy federal structure, where the BJP currently dominates a fragmented opposition.
