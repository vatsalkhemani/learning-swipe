# The Standard Model: physics' best theory of everything except gravity

## What it is and why it matters

The Standard Model is the mathematical theory that explains what matter is made of and how three of the four fundamental forces work. It was assembled piece by piece between roughly 1960 and 1975, then completed by the discovery of the **Higgs boson at CERN in 2012**. Within its scope it is the most precise theory in the history of science. Predictions match experiments to 10 or more decimal places. But it also has gaping holes. It says nothing about gravity. It does not explain dark matter or dark energy, which make up 95% of the universe. So you have the strange situation where physicists call the theory both "fantastically successful" and "obviously incomplete" in the same breath. That tension is where most of fundamental physics lives right now.

## The history that led to it

- **Ernest Rutherford, 1911.** Discovered the atomic nucleus by firing alpha particles at gold foil and seeing some bounce back. The atom was mostly empty space, with a dense positive core.
- **Niels Bohr, 1913.** Quantized the atom. Electrons jump between fixed orbits, emitting or absorbing light of specific energies. Explained why hydrogen's light spectrum had the lines it did.
- **The 1920s quantum revolution.** **Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrodinger, Max Born, Paul Dirac** built quantum mechanics. Particles became probability waves. Classical intuition died.
- **Paul Dirac, 1928.** Wrote an equation that combined quantum mechanics with special relativity. It predicted antimatter as a mathematical necessity. **Carl Anderson** found the first antimatter particle, the positron, in 1932 in cosmic rays.
- **QED, 1940s-1950s.** **Richard Feynman, Julian Schwinger, and Sin-Itiro Tomonaga** independently built quantum electrodynamics, the quantum theory of the electromagnetic force. They shared the **1965 Nobel**. QED's predictions for the electron's magnetic moment agree with experiment to 12 decimal places. It is the template every other piece of the Standard Model copies.
- **The 1960s and 70s build-out.** Quarks were proposed by **Murray Gell-Mann** and **George Zweig** in 1964. The weak and electromagnetic forces were unified by **Sheldon Glashow, Abdus Salam, and Steven Weinberg**. The strong force theory (QCD) followed. By the mid 1970s the framework was essentially complete on paper.

## The particle zoo, organized

The Standard Model has **17 fundamental particles**, sorted into three groups.

- **Six quarks.** They come in pairs called generations: **up + down** (first), **charm + strange** (second), **top + bottom** (third). Quarks make up protons (two ups + one down) and neutrons (two downs + one up). The first generation makes ordinary matter. The other two are heavier copies that decay quickly. Nobody knows why there are three generations.
- **Six leptons.** Three charged ones, **electron + muon + tau**, are essentially heavier copies of each other. Three **neutrinos**, almost massless and barely interacting with anything. Neutrinos pass through Earth as if it were not there.
- **Four force-carrier particles (gauge bosons).** The **photon** carries the electromagnetic force. The **W and Z bosons** carry the weak force, which is responsible for some kinds of radioactive decay. The **gluon** carries the strong force, which binds quarks into protons and neutrons.
- **The Higgs boson.** The odd one out. Not a force carrier but the particle associated with the Higgs field, which is what gives the other particles their mass.

## The three forces it explains

- **Electromagnetism.** Light, magnets, chemistry, electricity, every interaction between everyday matter. Infinite range, transmitted by photons.
- **The weak force.** Causes certain types of radioactive decay and powers the first step of fusion inside the Sun. Very short range, transmitted by W and Z bosons. The "weak" name is misleading. It is essential.
- **The strong force.** Holds quarks inside protons and neutrons, and holds those together inside the atomic nucleus. The strongest of the four forces but only acts at extremely short range.
- **Gravity is missing.** This is the headline problem. We have **Einstein's general relativity** for gravity, and we have the Standard Model for everything else, and the two are not compatible.

## The Higgs mechanism

- **Peter Higgs** (along with **Francois Englert and Robert Brout** independently) proposed in **1964** that space is filled with a field, now called the **Higgs field**. Particles interact with this field, and that interaction is what we experience as mass. Massless particles like photons do not interact with it. Massive particles like quarks and electrons do.
- The Higgs boson is the particle you get when you excite the Higgs field. Finding it was the missing piece of the Standard Model.
- It took 48 years and roughly **$5 billion** to find. The **Large Hadron Collider (LHC)** at CERN, a 27-kilometer ring under the Switzerland-France border, finally produced confirmed Higgs bosons in **July 2012**. Higgs and Englert won the **2013 Nobel** the next year.

## What is missing

The Standard Model is incomplete. The list of things it cannot explain is large.

- **Dark matter.** About 27% of the universe is made of something that has gravity but does not interact with light. The Standard Model has no candidate for what it is.
- **Dark energy.** Another 68% of the universe is a mysterious energy pushing space itself to expand faster. The Standard Model says nothing about it.
- **Gravity.** Not in the theory at all. Combining gravity with quantum mechanics is the holy grail. String theory and loop quantum gravity are two unproven attempts.
- **Neutrino masses.** The original Standard Model said neutrinos had zero mass. Experiments since 1998 showed they have tiny masses. The theory has been patched but not really explained.
- **Why three generations?** Why does each particle come in three heavier copies that decay almost immediately? No one knows.
- **Matter-antimatter asymmetry.** The Big Bang should have produced equal amounts. The universe is almost entirely matter. The Standard Model cannot account for the imbalance.

## What to carry away

- The Standard Model is a 17-particle, three-force theory that works ridiculously well within its scope and obviously cannot be the final answer.
- The Higgs discovery in 2012 closed the chapter that started with Rutherford in 1911. A century of work, finished.
- The next chapter is what dark matter is, why gravity does not fit, and whether there is a deeper theory underneath. Nobody has a proven candidate yet.
- A theory can be the best one humans have ever built and still be a stepping stone. That is the Standard Model.
