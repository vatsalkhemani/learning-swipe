# The 20th century in one read: world wars, Cold War, decolonization

## What the 20th century was and why it matters

The 20th century was the most violent, transformative stretch in human history. Roughly 100 million people died in its wars. European empires that had ruled most of the planet in 1900 had collapsed by 1970. Two new superpowers emerged, faced off for 45 years, and one of them disappeared. The shape of today's borders, alliances, institutions, and grievances was almost entirely set between 1914 and 1991. If you understand this century, you understand why Ukraine and Gaza make sense to the people fighting them, why the UN looks the way it does, and why the US has bases in 80 countries.

## The pre-1914 world

- In 1900 the British Empire ruled about a quarter of the planet, the French held most of West Africa and Indochina, the Germans were late and angry about it, and the Ottomans were a fading power running the Middle East.
- This was the era of **Pax Britannica**, a relative peace held together by British naval dominance, the gold standard, and free trade.
- Globalization was already deep. Capital and people moved more freely than they would again until the 1990s.
- Underneath it: arms races, nationalist grievances in the Balkans, and a tangle of alliances that would turn one assassination into a continental war.

## World War I, 1914-18

- Triggered by the assassination of Austrian Archduke **Franz Ferdinand** in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914. The alliance system did the rest.
- Industrialized killing on a scale nobody anticipated. **Machine guns**, artillery, poison gas, and trench warfare across France and Belgium produced a stalemate measured in inches and corpses.
- About **20 million dead**, half of them civilians once the Spanish flu hit. The Battle of the Somme alone cost a million casualties.
- The US entered in **April 1917** after German submarines targeted American shipping. American manpower broke the deadlock.
- The **Treaty of Versailles** in 1919 stripped Germany of territory, capped its military, and imposed reparations its economy could not pay. **John Maynard Keynes** quit the British delegation and wrote a book predicting the peace would cause the next war. He was right.

## The interwar period

- The **Spanish flu** of 1918-20 killed roughly 50 million people worldwide, more than the war itself.
- The Russian Revolution of 1917 brought the Bolsheviks to power. By the early 1920s the **Soviet Union** existed as a permanent ideological alternative to Western capitalism.
- The **1929 stock market crash** in New York triggered the Great Depression. By 1933 a quarter of American workers were unemployed. German unemployment hit 30%.
- Economic collapse plus Versailles humiliation fed fascism. **Mussolini** took Italy in 1922. **Hitler** became German chancellor in January 1933. Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931.

## World War II, 1939-45

- Hitler and Stalin signed a non-aggression pact in August 1939, secretly carving up Eastern Europe. Germany invaded Poland on September 1. Britain and France declared war.
- Hitler turned on Stalin in June 1941 (**Operation Barbarossa**), opening the Eastern Front, which would become the bloodiest theater in human history. The Soviets lost about 27 million people.
- The **Holocaust** killed approximately 6 million Jews, alongside Roma, disabled people, gay men, political prisoners, and Soviet POWs. Industrial-scale genocide at Auschwitz, Treblinka, Sobibor.
- The Pacific theater opened with Japan attacking **Pearl Harbor** on December 7, 1941. Island-by-island campaign across the Pacific.
- The US dropped atomic bombs on **Hiroshima** (August 6, 1945) and **Nagasaki** (August 9). Roughly 200,000 dead, mostly civilians. Japan surrendered on August 15.
- Total dead, roughly 70-85 million. About 3% of the 1940 world population.

## The postwar order

- **United Nations** founded in San Francisco in 1945, replacing the failed League of Nations.
- **Bretton Woods** in 1944 created the IMF, the World Bank, and a dollar-anchored monetary system.
- **NATO** formed in 1949 to bind the US to Europe's defense against the Soviets. The Warsaw Pact answered in 1955.
- Wave of **decolonization**. India and Pakistan independent in 1947 (partition killed about a million people). Chinese Communists won their civil war in 1949. Most of Africa freed itself in the late 1950s and early 1960s. By 1970, the European empires were gone.

## The Cold War

- Not one war but a 45-year standoff. Nuclear stalemate, proxy wars, espionage, ideological competition.
- **Berlin Wall** went up in 1961, splitting the city overnight and becoming the Cold War's most visible symbol.
- **Cuban Missile Crisis** in October 1962 came the closest humanity got to nuclear war. Thirteen days. Soviet missiles came out of Cuba, American missiles quietly came out of Turkey.
- **Vietnam** from 1955 to 1975. The US lost 58,000 dead, Vietnam lost roughly 3 million. The war shattered American confidence at home.
- Proxy conflicts in Angola, Mozambique, Nicaragua, El Salvador, Afghanistan, the Horn of Africa. The Global South paid the bills.
- The Soviet system buckled under economic stagnation and an arms race it couldn't fund. **Mikhail Gorbachev** opened things up with glasnost and perestroika in the late 1980s.
- Berlin Wall fell in **November 1989**. Soviet Union dissolved on December 25, 1991.

## The "end of history" moment

- **Francis Fukuyama** published *The End of History* in 1992. The thesis: liberal democracy had won the argument with every rival system. There was nothing serious left to fight about.
- The 1990s seemed to confirm it. NATO expanded east. China joined the WTO in 2001. The internet went global. Democracies multiplied.
- It was premature. **9/11** in 2001 brought a new conflict frame. The 2008 financial crisis cracked confidence in the system. Putin's Russia turned revisionist. China grew faster than anyone expected and didn't liberalize. Populism rose across the democracies.

## What to carry away

- The 20th century's borders, institutions, and grievances are still ours. Versailles fed Hitler. Hitler created the postwar order. The postwar order created the world we live in.
- Three forces ran the century: industrial-scale violence, ideological competition between liberalism and its rivals, and the unwinding of European empire.
- The "end of history" lasted about a decade. We are now in something new without a clean name. Most analysts call it multipolar, and most of the major flashpoints (Ukraine, Taiwan, the Middle East) sit on Cold War fault lines that never fully closed.
- One sentence: the 20th century industrialized killing, ended European empire, and left two superpowers facing off until one of them quit. Everything else is detail.
