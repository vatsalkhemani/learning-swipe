# China's rise: from Deng Xiaoping to today

## What happened and why it matters

In 1978 China was poorer per capita than most of sub-Saharan Africa. By 2024 it was the world's second-largest economy, the largest manufacturer, and the only serious peer competitor the United States has faced in 30 years. About 800 million people moved out of poverty in this stretch. No country has ever transformed this fast at this scale. The question hanging over the next decade is whether China overtakes the US, or peaks before it does. Either way, every supply chain, every alliance, and every technology race you read about traces back to this story.

## The starting point

- **Mao Zedong** founded the People's Republic in 1949 after winning the civil war against the Nationalists. His rule ran 1949 to 1976.
- The **Great Leap Forward** (1958-62) was Mao's push to industrialize overnight through collectivized agriculture and backyard steel furnaces. It produced one of the worst famines in history. Roughly 30 million dead.
- The **Cultural Revolution** (1966-76) was Mao's purge of perceived enemies inside the Communist Party. Schools closed, intellectuals were sent to the countryside, neighbors denounced neighbors. Another roughly 1-2 million dead, and a generation lost their education.
- By Mao's death in **September 1976**, China was poor, isolated, traumatized, and decades behind the West technologically.

## Deng Xiaoping opens the door

- **Deng Xiaoping** consolidated power by **1978**. He had been purged twice under Mao and came back the third time as the most consequential leader of the century.
- His pragmatism is captured in his famous line: "It doesn't matter whether a cat is black or white, so long as it catches mice."
- He kept the Communist Party in charge politically and let markets run economically. The model later got called "socialism with Chinese characteristics."
- **Special Economic Zones** opened in 1980. **Shenzhen** went from a fishing village of 30,000 to a megacity of 17 million in three decades. Foreign capital, low-cost labor, export manufacturing.
- Agricultural decollectivization let farmers sell surplus. Rural incomes jumped. The household responsibility system did more to lift people out of poverty than any program in history.
- **Tiananmen Square** in June 1989 was the limit Deng drew. Pro-democracy protests in the capital, tanks sent in, hundreds to thousands killed. The signal: economic opening yes, political opening no.

## The inflection: WTO accession

- China joined the **World Trade Organization** in **December 2001**. This was the moment the rise became irreversible.
- Within a decade China was the workshop of the world. By 2010 it had overtaken Japan as the second-largest economy.
- American manufacturing employment dropped by roughly 6 million jobs in the 2000s. Economists later called this the "China shock." It quietly reshaped American politics.
- China built infrastructure at a pace nobody had seen. More high-speed rail miles than the rest of the world combined. Cities of 10 million nobody outside China had heard of.

## The Hu Jintao era, 2003-13

- **Hu Jintao** was a technocrat. His era pushed the line "peaceful rise" to reassure neighbors and the US that a strong China didn't threaten anyone.
- Growth averaged about 10% a year. The 2008 Olympics in Beijing was the coming-out party.
- China weathered the **2008 global financial crisis** with a massive stimulus, about $586 billion. It worked but loaded the banking system with debt that still hasn't fully cleared.
- Underneath the technocratic surface, corruption inside the Party metastasized.

## Xi Jinping consolidates, 2013 to today

- **Xi Jinping** took over in 2012-13. He moved fast.
- The **anti-corruption campaign** netted over a million officials. Real anti-corruption, and a convenient way to remove rivals. Both at once.
- In **March 2018** the National People's Congress removed presidential term limits. Xi can rule for life.
- **Belt and Road Initiative** announced in 2013. Chinese-financed infrastructure across Asia, Africa, Latin America. Roughly $1 trillion committed. Some of it built genuine connectivity. Some of it created debt traps.
- **Hong Kong**, promised "one country, two systems" until 2047, was effectively absorbed by 2020 after the National Security Law crushed the 2019 protest movement.
- **Xinjiang**: mass internment camps for Uyghur Muslims, estimated 1 million-plus detained, forced labor in cotton and solar panel supply chains.
- **COVID** broke out in Wuhan in late 2019. The origin remains contested (zoonotic vs lab leak from the Wuhan Institute of Virology). China's zero-COVID lockdowns lasted into late 2022 and damaged growth.

## Current pressures

- **Demographics.** Population peaked in 2022 and is now shrinking. The one-child policy (1980-2015) means the workforce contracts every year from here. China gets old before it gets rich.
- **Real estate.** **Evergrande** defaulted in 2021. Country Garden in 2023. Property was about 25-30% of GDP and a third of household wealth. The deflation is grinding.
- **Tech crackdown.** **Jack Ma** disappeared from public view after criticizing regulators in October 2020. Ant Group's IPO got killed days before launch. Didi got hammered after listing in New York. The signal: no private company is bigger than the Party.
- **Taiwan.** Beijing claims Taiwan as a province. Xi has told the military to be ready for invasion by 2027. The US has strategic ambiguity, but most analysts think it would intervene.
- **Tech decoupling.** The US blocked advanced semiconductors and chipmaking equipment to China starting under Trump, escalated under Biden. The CHIPS Act in 2022 was the American response. China's response is massive investment in domestic chips, with mixed results.

## The big question

- **Does China overtake the US, or peak before it does?**
- The bull case: China still has room to urbanize, has dominant positions in EVs, batteries, solar, and shipbuilding, and is closing the gap on AI.
- The bear case: aging population, property bust, deflation, capital flight, frozen reform, no political release valve, and a leader who chose control over growth.
- Most serious analysts now think China has peaked or will peak in the late 2020s, well short of US GDP. That doesn't make it weak. It makes it a powerful, declining-momentum rival, which historically is the most dangerous kind.

## What to carry away

- Deng made the choice that everything else flowed from: market economy, one-party state.
- WTO accession in 2001 was the inflection. Xi consolidating in 2018 was the second inflection, in the opposite direction.
- The era of fast Chinese growth is probably over. The era of US-China competition is probably just starting.
- One sentence: China spent 40 years pulling 800 million people out of poverty under Deng's bet, and the next 20 years will tell us whether Xi's reversal of that bet works.
