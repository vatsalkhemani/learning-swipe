# The Middle East 101: Israel-Palestine, Iran-Saudi, oil politics

## What the Middle East actually is and why it matters

The modern Middle East is what's left after the Ottoman Empire collapsed in 1918 and Europeans drew lines on a map. Inside those borders sit three conflicts that all bleed into each other: Israel and the Palestinians, Iran versus Saudi Arabia (with the older Sunni-Shia split underneath), and the oil politics that pull every great power into the region. It matters because the world economy still runs on oil that mostly comes from here, because the US has spent more on wars here than anywhere else since Vietnam, and because the conflicts have a way of detonating outward, as October 7 reminded everyone.

## The Ottoman collapse and the lines on the map

- The **Ottoman Empire** ran most of the Middle East for 400 years until it picked the losing side in World War I.
- The **Sykes-Picot Agreement** in May 1916 was a secret British-French deal to carve up Ottoman lands after the war. France took what is now Syria and Lebanon. Britain took Iraq, Jordan, and Palestine. Lines drawn without much reference to who actually lived there.
- The **Balfour Declaration** in November 1917 committed Britain to support "a national home for the Jewish people in Palestine." About 600,000 Arabs lived there at the time and roughly 60,000 Jews. The contradiction between this and parallel British promises to Arab leaders is the original wound.
- The League of Nations gave Britain a mandate over Palestine in 1922. Jewish immigration accelerated through the 1920s and 1930s, especially as Nazi Germany rose.

## Israel-Palestine, the long arc

- **May 14, 1948**: Israel declares independence. Five Arab states invade the next day. Israel wins.
- The war displaced about **700,000 Palestinians**, called the **Nakba** (catastrophe) by Palestinians and the War of Independence by Israelis. Same event, two names, two narratives, every detail still contested 76 years later.
- **Six-Day War, June 1967**: Israel hit Egypt, Jordan, and Syria simultaneously. In six days it took the **West Bank** from Jordan, **Gaza** and **Sinai** from Egypt, and the **Golan Heights** from Syria. The occupation that began here still defines the conflict.
- **Yom Kippur War, October 1973**: Egypt and Syria attacked on the Jewish holiday. Israel was caught off guard but rallied. The shock pushed Egypt eventually toward peace.
- **Camp David Accords, September 1978**: **Anwar Sadat**, **Menachem Begin**, and Jimmy Carter negotiated 13 days at the presidential retreat. Egypt got Sinai back. Israel got peace with its largest Arab neighbor. Sadat was assassinated in 1981 for it.
- **Oslo Accords, 1993**: **Yitzhak Rabin** and **Yasser Arafat** shook hands on the White House lawn. Created the Palestinian Authority, promised a path to two states. Rabin was assassinated by an Israeli extremist in 1995.
- **Second Intifada, 2000-05**: Palestinian uprising, suicide bombings in Israeli cities, harsh Israeli response. Killed about 1,000 Israelis and 3,000 Palestinians. Buried the Oslo process.
- **Settlements**: about 700,000 Israelis now live in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. Most of the international community considers this illegal. Israel disputes that. The settlements make a contiguous Palestinian state harder to draw every year.

## The Sunni-Shia split

- The split goes back to **632 CE**, the year **Muhammad** died. The question was succession.
- **Sunnis** (today about 85-90% of Muslims) accepted that the community could choose the successor. **Abu Bakr**, Muhammad's father-in-law, became the first caliph.
- **Shia** (today about 10-15%) believed leadership belonged to Muhammad's bloodline through his cousin and son-in-law **Ali**. When Ali's son **Husayn** was killed at **Karbala in 680 CE**, the split became permanent. Shia commemorate his death every year on Ashura.
- For most of history this was a theological difference, not a war. The modern weaponization is recent, driven by the 1979 Iranian Revolution and the 2003 US invasion of Iraq.

## Iran versus Saudi Arabia

- **Iran**: Shia, Persian (not Arab), revolutionary since 1979. The **Islamic Revolution** overthrew the US-backed Shah and brought **Ayatollah Khomeini** to power. Hostage crisis at the US embassy ran 444 days.
- **Saudi Arabia**: Sunni, Arab, monarchy, US ally, sits on a quarter of the world's proven oil. Home to **Mecca** and **Medina**, the two holiest cities in Islam. The royal family's legitimacy comes from a deal with Wahhabi clerics in the 18th century.
- The two have run a regional cold war for 45 years through proxies:
  - **Yemen**: Saudi-led coalition versus Iran-backed Houthis since 2015. One of the worst humanitarian crises in the world.
  - **Syria**: Iran backed Assad, Saudi backed Sunni opposition. About 500,000 dead in the civil war that started in 2011.
  - **Lebanon**: **Hezbollah**, founded 1982, is Iran's most capable proxy. Armed Shia movement with its own army, often stronger than Lebanon's.
  - **Iraq**: Iran's influence grew massively after the US toppled Saddam in 2003.
- **March 2023**: Saudi Arabia and Iran restored diplomatic ties in a deal brokered by **China**. Significant moment. Signaled to Washington that the Gulf was hedging.

## Oil politics

- Commercial oil was first struck in **Persia in 1908**. Standard practice for the next 50 years was that Western companies controlled the oil and host countries got royalties.
- **OPEC** formed in September 1960 in Baghdad. Five founding members. The cartel started slowly.
- **1973 oil embargo**: After the US backed Israel in the Yom Kippur War, Arab OPEC states cut off oil to the US and quadrupled prices. Long gas lines in America, recession across the West.
- The embargo proved that oil was a geopolitical weapon. For the next 50 years, US foreign policy in the region has been built around keeping oil flowing.
- **US energy independence** through shale (the 2010s) changed the calculation. The US became the world's largest oil producer by 2018. Saudi Arabia matters less to the US, which is part of why the Saudis are hedging toward China.
- Oil is also why the Gulf monarchies have lasted. Massive rents fund massive subsidies and surveillance, no taxes, no representation.

## The current moment

- **October 7, 2023**: Hamas attacked from Gaza, killed about 1,200 Israelis, took roughly 250 hostages. The deadliest day for Jews since the Holocaust.
- Israel's response: full ground invasion of Gaza. As of mid-2025, Palestinian deaths reported at over 40,000, mostly women and children, with most of Gaza's housing destroyed.
- **South Africa** filed a **genocide case** against Israel at the International Court of Justice in January 2024. Case still proceeding.
- **Hezbollah** opened a northern front against Israel. Israel hit back hard, killed Hezbollah leader **Hassan Nasrallah** in September 2024. **Iran** and Israel exchanged direct strikes for the first time in April and October 2024.
- **Houthis** attacked shipping in the Red Sea starting late 2023. Roughly 12% of global trade routes through there. Shipping costs spiked.
- **Saudi-Israel normalization** had been in negotiation before October 7. Now on pause, possibly off the table.

## What to carry away

- The current conflicts sit on top of a 100-year-old colonial map and a 1,400-year-old religious split. Both still matter.
- 1948 (Israel's founding), 1967 (the occupation begins), 1979 (Iran becomes revolutionary), and 2003 (US topples Saddam, hands Iraq to Iran) are the dates that bend the curve.
- The US is less anchored to the region than it used to be because of shale. China is more anchored because it needs the oil. That is the strategic shift underneath everything.
- One sentence: the Middle East is what happened when Europeans drew lines through an Ottoman ruin, dropped a Jewish state into Arab land, and discovered the region was floating on oil.
