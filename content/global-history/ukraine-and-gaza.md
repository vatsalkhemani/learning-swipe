# Ukraine and Gaza: why current wars matter

## Why these two wars matter

Ukraine and Gaza are not just regional tragedies. They are the first major wars of the post-American-unipolar era, and the world is watching how they end. Nuclear powers back opposite sides. Supply chains and energy markets have moved. Politics in democracies has fractured around them. Authoritarian states (China most importantly) are studying what they can and can't do based on how the West responds. If you want to understand what the 2020s and 2030s are going to look like, you need to understand these two wars.

## Ukraine: the background

- **Ukraine declared independence on August 24, 1991**, weeks before the Soviet Union formally dissolved. For most of the 20th century it had been a Soviet republic, before that part of the Russian Empire.
- The country has been pulled between Russia and the West ever since. Eastern Ukraine is more Russian-speaking and Russia-leaning. Western Ukraine looks toward Europe.
- **Budapest Memorandum, 1994**: Ukraine gave up its nuclear weapons (it had inherited the world's third-largest arsenal from the USSR) in exchange for security assurances from the US, UK, and Russia. The Russians later treated this as non-binding.
- **NATO Bucharest summit, April 2008**: NATO declared Ukraine and Georgia "will become members." No timeline, no concrete path. Putin treated this as a red line. Russia invaded Georgia four months later.
- **Maidan revolution, late 2013 to February 2014**: protests in Kyiv against pro-Russian president **Viktor Yanukovych**, who had backed out of an EU association agreement. After snipers killed roughly 100 protesters, Yanukovych fled to Russia.
- **March 2014**: Russia annexed **Crimea** after a sham referendum. Same month, Russia fomented separatist movements in the **Donbas** (Donetsk and Luhansk regions). Low-grade war ran 2014-22, killing about 14,000.

## The full Russian invasion

- **February 24, 2022**: Russia launched a full invasion from three directions, targeting Kyiv from the north. The plan was a three-day decapitation strike.
- The plan failed. Ukrainian resistance was much stiffer than Moscow expected. **Volodymyr Zelenskyy**, a former comedian elected president in 2019, stayed in Kyiv and became a wartime symbol.
- Russian forces withdrew from the north by April, refocused on the east and south. Atrocities at **Bucha** (a Kyiv suburb where Russian forces executed civilians) became a turning point in Western public opinion.
- Ukraine's counteroffensive in fall 2022 took back Kharkiv region and Kherson city.
- 2023 counteroffensive bogged down against Russian fortifications. The war became attritional.
- **Current status (mid-2025)**: stalemate. Russia controls about 20% of Ukrainian territory. Casualties on both sides estimated in the hundreds of thousands. Trump's return has put Western support in question.

## Why Putin's stated reasons matter (even though they're contested)

- Putin's official justification: stop **NATO expansion**, and "denazify" Ukraine.
- The NATO point is contested but real to Moscow. The Kremlin has said since the 1990s that NATO moving east violated assurances given to Gorbachev. Most Western historians say no formal promise was made, but informal assurances were. Either way, Russia treats it as betrayal.
- The "denazification" framing is propaganda. Ukraine has a small far-right element (Azov Battalion most prominently), but it's a Jewish-led democracy whose president was elected with 73%.
- The deeper Russian view, articulated in Putin's July 2021 essay: Ukraine isn't a real country, it's an artificial creation, and its people are really Russians who got confused. This is the actual war aim. Denial of Ukrainian existence.

## The Western response

- **Sanctions** at unprecedented scale. Russian central bank reserves frozen (about $300 billion). Russian banks cut off from SWIFT. Trade restrictions across energy, tech, luxury goods.
- **Weapons** to Ukraine, but no NATO troops. Roughly $200 billion in Western aid by 2025. Equipment kept escalating: Javelins, then HIMARS, then tanks, then F-16s.
- **NATO unity** held up far better than expected. **Sweden** and **Finland** both joined NATO (Finland in 2023, Sweden in 2024), doubling NATO's border with Russia.
- **Energy**: Europe spent 2022 frantically weaning off Russian gas. Painful, especially for Germany. LNG imports from the US filled some of the gap. Europe never went dark.

## Gaza: the background

- **Hamas** won Palestinian legislative elections in 2006 and took full control of Gaza in 2007 after fighting the rival Fatah faction.
- Israel and Egypt imposed a **blockade** of Gaza from 2007. Most goods and people couldn't enter or leave. 2.2 million people in a strip of land 25 miles long and 7 miles wide.
- Periodic wars: 2008-9, 2012, 2014, 2021. Hamas rockets versus Israeli airstrikes. Each round killed hundreds to thousands of Palestinians and a smaller number of Israelis.
- The conflict had been dropping down the international agenda. Abraham Accords (2020) normalized Israel-UAE and Israel-Bahrain ties. Saudi normalization was being negotiated.

## October 7 and after

- **October 7, 2023**: Hamas militants broke through the Gaza fence, attacked Israeli border communities and a music festival. Killed about **1,200 Israelis**, including over 800 civilians. Took roughly **250 hostages** back to Gaza. Deadliest day for Jews since the Holocaust.
- Israel's response: full air campaign, then ground invasion of Gaza. Stated goal was to destroy Hamas and free hostages.
- **Palestinian deaths**: over 40,000 reported by mid-2025, mostly women and children, with disputes about combatant counts. Roughly 70% of Gaza's buildings damaged or destroyed.
- **Humanitarian crisis**: famine warnings, hospitals out of service, displacement of most of the population multiple times.
- **International Court of Justice**, January 2024: South Africa filed a **genocide case** against Israel. Case ongoing. The ICJ issued provisional measures asking Israel to prevent acts of genocide and allow humanitarian aid.
- **International Criminal Court**, November 2024: arrest warrants issued for **Benjamin Netanyahu**, defense minister Yoav Gallant, and Hamas military leaders. The first time the ICC issued warrants for a sitting Western-aligned leader.

## Regional spillover

- **Hezbollah** in Lebanon opened a northern front, exchanged fire with Israel for a year, displaced about 60,000 Israelis from the north. Israel hit back hard in fall 2024, killed Hezbollah leader **Hassan Nasrallah** in September 2024.
- **Iran and Israel** exchanged direct missile and drone strikes in April and October 2024. First direct exchanges in the decades-long shadow war.
- **Houthis** in Yemen attacked shipping in the **Red Sea** starting November 2023. Major shipping routes diverted around Africa. Shipping costs spiked.
- **Syria** dimension: Assad's regime collapsed in December 2024, partly because Russia (busy in Ukraine) and Iran (taking hits in Lebanon and from Israel) couldn't prop him up. **HTS**, an Islamist rebel group, took Damascus.

## Why both wars matter beyond their regions

- **Nuclear powers backing different sides.** Russia is a nuclear power directly fighting. The US is the major backer of the other side. In Gaza, Israel is nuclear-armed (undeclared), and Iran is close. The escalation risks are not theoretical.
- **Supply chains and commodities**. Wheat prices spiked because of Ukraine (Ukraine and Russia together supplied about 30% of global wheat exports). Energy markets reshuffled. Shipping costs jumped after the Red Sea attacks.
- **Populist political fallout**. Both wars have split democracies. American campuses erupted over Gaza in 2024. European governments have fallen partly over Ukraine fatigue. Trump's return brought a sharp reorientation on both wars.
- **Signal to authoritarian states**. **China is watching Taiwan**. The key questions: Can the West sustain a long war? Will sanctions actually work against a major economy? Will the US fight or just supply? The answers in Ukraine will shape Beijing's calculation on Taiwan.
- **End of the post-1945 norm** that you don't change borders by force. Russia is openly trying to. The precedent matters.

## What to carry away

- Ukraine is a war over whether borders can be changed by force in 2020s Europe. Gaza is a war over an unresolved 1948 conflict that the world had stopped paying attention to.
- Both are testing the limits of the post-Cold War order: nuclear taboo, territorial integrity norm, international institutions like the ICJ and ICC, Western unity, sanctions as a tool.
- China is watching both as case studies for what it could get away with on Taiwan.
- The 2020s won't be defined by these wars exactly, but by the precedents they set.
- One sentence: Ukraine and Gaza are where the post-American world order is being negotiated, with real bodies and real consequences, by everyone except the people doing the fighting.
