# Exercise: cardio, strength, mobility, and what actually matters past 30

## Why exercise matters more as you age

In your twenties you can get away with neglect. The body forgives you. Past 30 the bill starts arriving. **Sarcopenia**, the age-related loss of muscle mass, kicks in around 30 and accelerates each decade. Bone density peaks in your late 20s and starts declining. The cardiovascular system loses capacity if it isn't asked to work. None of this is a crisis at 35. By 65 it's the difference between an independent life and a dependent one. The strongest finding in modern longevity science is that exercise outperforms every drug and every supplement for extending healthspan. **Peter Attia**, the longevity physician who wrote *Outlive* (2023), has gone on record saying VO2 max, a measure of cardiovascular fitness, is the single best predictor of all-cause mortality we have. Better than smoking status. Better than blood pressure.

## The four pillars

- **Cardiovascular.** Heart and lung capacity. Lowers all-cause mortality more than any other input. The body's ability to deliver oxygen to working muscles, measured by VO2 max. People in the top quartile of VO2 max for their age have roughly half the mortality risk of those in the bottom quartile. The gap between bottom and second-from-bottom is the biggest single jump in health gained from any intervention.
- **Strength.** Resistance training. Preserves muscle and bone. Drives metabolism. Maintains balance, which becomes the single biggest fall-risk factor past 65. Grip strength alone is a startlingly good predictor of longevity.
- **Mobility and stability.** Joints moving through their full range, controlled. Different from flexibility, which is passive range. Mobility prevents injury and becomes existential past 50, when small falls turn into hip fractures that change life trajectories.
- **Flexibility.** Passive range of motion. Less critical than Instagram suggests. Stretching before lifting doesn't prevent injury. Stretching does help mobility when done as deliberate work.

## The minimum effective dose

- **Roughly 150 minutes per week of moderate cardio**, per the WHO and US guidelines. Brisk walking counts.
- **Two to three strength sessions per week**, covering all major muscle groups. Compound movements like squats, hinges, pushes, pulls, and carries do most of the work.
- **Some mobility work**, even 10 minutes a few times a week. Hips, ankles, shoulders, thoracic spine.
- This is the floor, not the ceiling. The dose-response curve for cardio keeps improving up to around 300-400 minutes a week, then flattens.

## Zone 2 cardio

- The slow steady-state cardio that's been having a long moment, championed by Attia and the exercise physiologist **Inigo San Millan**.
- Defined as the intensity at which you can hold a conversation but it's mildly uncomfortable. Heart rate roughly 60-70% of max. Lactate stays low.
- Why it matters: it builds **mitochondrial density**, the cellular machinery that converts fuel to energy. Mitochondrial health is now considered central to metabolic health and longevity.
- Practical dose: 2-4 sessions per week, 45-60 minutes each. Walking on an incline, cycling, rowing, easy jogging. Most untrained people overcook the intensity and miss the adaptation.

## HIIT and the top end

- **High-intensity interval training.** Short bursts at or near max effort, with recovery. Norwegian 4x4 protocol is the most studied: 4 minutes hard, 3 minutes easy, repeated 4 times.
- HIIT efficiently builds VO2 max. Strong evidence, time-efficient.
- The catch: HIIT is harder to recover from. One or two sessions a week, on top of Zone 2, is the standard recommendation. Pure HIIT diets burn people out.

## Strength training past 40

- **Mike Israetel**, an exercise scientist with a popular YouTube presence, makes the case that strength training matters more than steady-state cardio for longevity past 40. The argument: sarcopenia is the dominant aging signal, and only resistance training reverses it.
- Most people don't need elaborate programs. Three full-body sessions a week, basic compound lifts, progressive overload (add weight, reps, or sets over time) covers it.
- Protein matters here. Roughly 0.7-1g per pound of bodyweight per day for active adults. Older adults need the upper end because muscle protein synthesis becomes less responsive with age.
- Form matters, but the obsession with perfect form prevents many people from ever starting. Reasonable form, sustained for years, beats perfect form abandoned in two months.

## What doesn't matter as much as Instagram suggests

- **Pre-workout stretching.** Doesn't prevent injury. Static stretching cold may slightly reduce power output.
- **Perfect form on every rep.** Some grinding on the last reps of a hard set is normal and probably useful.
- **Fasted cardio for fat loss.** Calories over the day matter, not the fasting window.
- **Complicated periodized programs** for non-athletes. Most people benefit more from showing up consistently than optimizing the program.
- **Supplements**, with a few exceptions. Creatine is real and well-studied. Protein powder is convenient food, not magic. Most pre-workouts are caffeine and hype.

## What actually matters

- **Consistency over years.** The person who trains 3 times a week for 20 years beats the person who trains 6 times a week for 6 months.
- **Progressive overload.** The body adapts to whatever you ask of it. Stop asking for more and adaptation stops.
- **Recovery.** Sleep, food, and time between hard sessions. Training is the stimulus, recovery is where adaptation happens.
- **The basics done well.** Cardio you'll actually do, strength on compound lifts, some mobility, enough food, enough sleep.

## What to carry away

- Move every day. Lift heavy two or three times a week. Build a Zone 2 base. Sprinkle in some hard intervals.
- VO2 max and strength are the two metrics worth tracking past 30. They predict your independence at 75 better than almost anything else.
- The exercise you'll do beats the optimal program you won't.
- In one sentence: exercise is the closest thing to a longevity drug we have, the dose is moderate but consistent, and the cost of skipping it compounds quietly until one day it doesn't.
