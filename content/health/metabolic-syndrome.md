# Metabolic syndrome: the cluster of conditions reshaping how we think about disease

## What is metabolic syndrome and why it matters

Metabolic syndrome isn't a single disease. It's a cluster of conditions that show up together and dramatically raise your risk of heart disease, stroke, diabetes, and a growing list of other illnesses. The common thread underneath the cluster is **insulin resistance**, which is when your cells stop responding properly to insulin. Roughly 35% of US adults have metabolic syndrome, and roughly 50% of adults over 60. Understanding it is understanding the upstream driver of most modern chronic disease, which is why doctors and researchers increasingly treat it as the central health problem of our time rather than as a footnote on a lab report.

## The five criteria

You have metabolic syndrome if you meet three or more of these:

- **Abdominal obesity** — waist over 40 inches in men, over 35 inches in women. Visceral fat (around organs) is the dangerous kind, not subcutaneous fat (under skin).
- **High blood pressure** — over 130/85.
- **High fasting blood sugar** — over 100 mg/dL.
- **High triglycerides** — over 150 mg/dL.
- **Low HDL ("good") cholesterol** — under 40 in men, under 50 in women.

None of these alone is catastrophic. Three together signal something is structurally wrong with how your body handles energy.

## The mechanism: insulin resistance

- Insulin is the hormone that tells cells to take glucose out of your blood.
- Over years of high refined-carb and sugar intake, cells become less responsive to insulin's signal.
- Your pancreas compensates by pumping out more insulin to keep blood sugar in range.
- Eventually the pancreas tires, insulin production can't keep up, and blood sugar starts rising. That's **type 2 diabetes**.
- Long before diabetes shows up, the high circulating insulin is already doing damage. Insulin drives fat storage (especially visceral), promotes inflammation, and acts as a growth factor on some tissues.

## What it actually causes

Insulin resistance and the cluster around it drive:

- **Cardiovascular disease** — the number one killer globally.
- **Type 2 diabetes** — once a disease of older adults, now common in 30-somethings and showing up in children.
- **Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease** — now the most common liver disease, affecting roughly 25% of adults globally, with no obvious symptoms until late stage.
- **Alzheimer's disease** — sometimes called "type 3 diabetes" because of how strongly insulin resistance in the brain correlates with risk.
- **Several cancers** — insulin is a growth signal, and high circulating levels appear to feed certain tumors.
- **Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)** — insulin resistance is central to its mechanism.

## Why it's everywhere now

- The food environment changed faster than human biology. Refined carbohydrates, added sugars, seed oils, and ultra-processed foods became cheap and ubiquitous over a few decades.
- Physical activity collapsed. Most jobs are sedentary. Most leisure is sedentary.
- The **Pima Indians of Arizona** became the canonical example. After being forced off traditional farming in the 19th century and onto government rations heavy in refined flour and sugar, their rate of type 2 diabetes climbed to over 50% of adults, one of the highest ever recorded. The Pima living in Mexico on traditional diets show normal rates. Same genes, different environment.
- **South Asians (Indians, Pakistanis, Bangladeshis)** carry particularly high metabolic syndrome risk at lower BMI than Europeans. Body composition differences (more visceral fat per pound of weight) plus genetic factors mean a South Asian at BMI 23 can be metabolically sicker than a European at BMI 28.

## What actually works

- **Diet** — cutting ultra-processed food, increasing fiber and protein, controlling refined carbs (especially sugar and refined flour). The specific diet matters less than the principles. Mediterranean, low-carb, whole-foods plant-based all work when followed.
- **Exercise** — both cardio and strength training. Muscle is metabolic tissue. Building it raises your resting glucose disposal capacity. Cardio improves mitochondrial function and insulin sensitivity directly.
- **Sleep** — poor sleep worsens insulin sensitivity within a few nights of bad nights. Chronic short sleep tracks closely with metabolic dysfunction.
- **Weight loss** — losing 5 to 10% of bodyweight reverses most metabolic syndrome markers in most people. You don't need to be lean. You need to be moving in the right direction.
- **GLP-1 agonists** (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro) — semaglutide was originally a diabetes drug, with weight loss as a side effect. Since 2022 these drugs have transformed treatment. Weight loss of 15-22% sustained while on the drug is common. Effects on cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and possibly Alzheimer's are being demonstrated in trial after trial.
- **Bariatric surgery** — for severe cases, still the most effective long-term intervention, with diabetes remission in a majority of patients.

## What doesn't work as well as marketed

- Targeted "detox" products and cleanses. Your liver and kidneys already do this.
- Most superfood claims. No single food fixes a systems-level problem.
- Spot training to lose belly fat. You can't choose where fat comes off.
- Cardio without dietary change. You can't outrun a bad diet.

## What to carry away

- Metabolic syndrome is the upstream driver behind a large fraction of modern chronic disease.
- The cause is mostly environmental, not genetic, even though genetics decides who breaks first.
- The interventions are unglamorous and well-known. The hard part is that the food environment is engineered against you.
- In one sentence: if you understand insulin resistance, you understand most of what's killing modern people, and most of what to do about it.
