# FAANG arcs: Meta, Apple, Amazon, Netflix, Google — what each actually is

## What FAANG is and why the arcs matter

FAANG is a shorthand coined by CNBC's Jim Cramer for the five US tech companies that came to define the 2010s stock market: **Facebook (now Meta)**, **Apple**, **Amazon**, **Netflix**, and **Google (now Alphabet)**. They are very different businesses pretending to be one category. Apple sells you a phone. Amazon runs a warehouse and a cloud. Netflix produces TV. Google reads the web. Meta runs your social graph. What links them is scale, network effects, and the fact that each one became a piece of infrastructure that other businesses now depend on. Understanding their individual arcs matters because the bundle is fraying. Antitrust pressure, the AI moment, and regulation are forcing each of them into a different next chapter, and the picture five years from now will not look like FAANG.

## Meta

- Founded as **Facebook** in **February 2004** in a Harvard dorm room by Mark Zuckerberg and a handful of classmates. Originally TheFacebook, a directory for Harvard students. Opened to other universities through 2004 and 2005.
- Bought Instagram in **2012** for about $1 billion in cash and stock. Bought WhatsApp in **2014** for $19 billion. Both acquisitions look like the best deals in tech history in hindsight.
- IPO in **May 2012** at $38 a share. Stock dropped immediately and did not recover for over a year. The story that turned it around was the mobile pivot.
- The **Cambridge Analytica scandal** broke in **March 2018**, when reporting by the Guardian and the New York Times showed that a researcher had pulled data on tens of millions of Facebook users and the data had been used by a political consulting firm. The scandal made data privacy a mainstream topic and triggered serious regulatory attention.
- Rebranded the parent company to **Meta** in **October 2021**, signaling a long bet on virtual and augmented reality. The Reality Labs division has lost tens of billions and Meta has shifted in 2023-24 to selling AI as the next platform instead.
- Current shape: a social networking and messaging empire, four products with more than a billion users each, with side bets on VR and AI. Most revenue is still advertising on Instagram and Facebook.

## Apple

- Founded in **April 1976** by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ron Wayne in a Los Altos garage.
- Jobs was forced out in **1985** after a power struggle with John Sculley, the CEO he had recruited from Pepsi. He spent the next decade running NeXT and buying Pixar.
- Apple nearly died in **1996**. Market share in single digits, no clear product line, weeks from bankruptcy according to later accounts. Apple bought NeXT in **December 1996** for $400 million, and Steve Jobs returned as advisor, then CEO.
- The recovery was a sequence of products. **iMac** in 1998. **iPod** in 2001. **iTunes Store** in 2003. **iPhone** in 2007, which is the single most important product launch of the 21st century. **iPad** in 2010. **App Store** in 2008, which became its own economy.
- Jobs died in **October 2011**. **Tim Cook** became CEO and ran a different company. Cook is an operations executive. He took Apple from one of the most innovative companies in the world to the most logistically formidable. The Apple supply chain is a competitive moat.
- Under Cook, **Services** has become the second largest line of business after the iPhone. App Store fees, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Pay. High-margin and growing.
- Current shape: a hardware company that is increasingly a services company, with an AI strategy that arrived late and an enormous installed base of over two billion active devices.

## Amazon

- Founded by **Jeff Bezos** in **1994** in a Seattle garage as an online bookstore. Bezos picked books because they were small, easy to ship, and there were millions of titles, more than any physical store could carry.
- The early years were unprofitable on purpose. Bezos used the famous shareholder letter format from 1997 onward to explain that Amazon was investing every dollar back into growth and that long-term thinking was the strategy.
- The decision that quietly changed everything was **AWS**, **Amazon Web Services**, launched in **2006**. S3 in March, EC2 in August. AWS was originally built to handle Amazon's own internal infrastructure problem and then opened up to outside customers. By the mid-2010s AWS was the most profitable part of the company and the dominant cloud platform in the world.
- The **Whole Foods acquisition in 2017** for $13.7 billion put Amazon into physical grocery in one move.
- Bezos stepped down as CEO in **July 2021**, replaced by **Andy Jassy**, the former head of AWS. The choice signaled that AWS, not retail, was now the center of gravity.
- Current shape: three businesses on one balance sheet. Retail at scale with thin margins. AWS as the most profitable cloud platform in the world. And **advertising**, now the third pillar and one of the fastest growing parts of the company because sellers pay Amazon to be found on Amazon.

## Netflix

- Founded in **1997** by Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph as a **DVD by mail** service. The legend that Hastings started it after a late fee on Apollo 13 is probably embellished, but it makes a good origin story.
- The streaming pivot started in **2007**, when Netflix launched **Watch Now**, letting subscribers stream a small library on their PCs. The library was small. The bet was on bandwidth catching up.
- **House of Cards** in **February 2013** marked the entry into original content. Netflix paid $100 million for two seasons up front and gave David Fincher creative freedom. The bet was that owning content, not just licensing it, was the long-term game.
- The split from DVD into a separate brand called **Qwikster** in 2011 was the worst executive decision in the company's history. Hastings reversed it within weeks but lost 800,000 subscribers and most of the company's market value.
- By the late 2010s Netflix was effectively a Hollywood studio that also happened to operate a streaming service. **Stranger Things, The Crown, Squid Game, Wednesday** all came out of Netflix originals.
- Around **2022** the growth narrative cracked. Subscriber losses, the launch of an ad-supported tier in late 2022, and a crackdown on password sharing in 2023. The company is profitable and large, but no longer the obvious growth story it was.
- Current shape: the largest pure-play streaming company in the world, competing directly with Disney+, Max, Amazon Prime Video, and Apple TV+, with a content budget bigger than most traditional studios.

## Google

- Founded by **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** at **Stanford** in **1998**. The original research project was about ranking web pages by who linked to them, which they called **PageRank**.
- The business model came in **2000-2003** when Google built **AdWords**, an auction-based ad system tied to search queries. This is one of the most lucrative inventions in the history of capitalism. It still generates the bulk of Google's revenue.
- Bought **Android** in **2005** for about $50 million. Andy Rubin's small startup became, by 2014, the operating system on most phones in the world. Google paid back the price of the acquisition many times over in defensive value against Apple.
- Bought **YouTube** in **2006** for $1.65 billion. Now one of the largest media platforms in the world.
- Reorganized into **Alphabet** in **August 2015**, with Google as one subsidiary and other bets like Waymo, Verily, and DeepMind as separate companies under a parent holding structure. The move was partly to clarify financials and partly to insulate Google from regulatory action against the moonshots.
- **DeepMind**, acquired in 2014, did the AlphaGo work that beat Lee Sedol in **2016**, a watershed moment in AI.
- Currently scrambling on AI. Google had transformer technology in 2017, published the foundational paper, and then watched OpenAI commercialize it first. The AI overview rollout in search has been clumsy. The Gemini family of models is competitive but late. Google's challenge is that AI threatens to disrupt the very ad-search model that built the company.

## What links them

- **Scale**. All five operate at a size where they are no longer optional infrastructure for some part of modern life.
- **Network effects and data**. Each one's product gets better the more people use it.
- **Becoming infrastructure**. Apple's App Store, Amazon's AWS, Google's search index, Meta's social graph, and Netflix's streaming stack are all places where other businesses sit on top.

## The current vulnerabilities

- **Regulation**. The US Department of Justice has active antitrust cases against Google. The EU has aggressive new laws, the Digital Markets Act and the Digital Services Act, aimed mostly at these five plus Microsoft.
- **Antitrust unwinding**. The Meta case to unwind the Instagram and WhatsApp acquisitions is ongoing. The Google search case may force divestment of Chrome or Android.
- **The AI moment**. Generative AI threatens the search ad model that built Google. It is reshaping content production at Netflix. It is opening up a new platform race in which Microsoft and OpenAI got a head start. The five companies that defined the 2010s may not all be the ones that define the 2030s.
