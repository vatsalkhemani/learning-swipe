# Christianity: the religion that became the West's default operating system

## What is Christianity and why it matters

Christianity is the world's largest religion, with about 2.4 billion followers, roughly 31% of humanity. It centers on the life, death, and claimed resurrection of **Jesus of Nazareth**, a Jewish teacher executed by Rome around 30-33 CE. Within four centuries it went from a persecuted sect to the official religion of the Roman Empire, and from there it shaped the legal codes, calendars, art, ethics, and assumptions of what we now call the West. Even people who never set foot in a church are living inside Christianity's residue: the seven-day week, the idea of universal human dignity, the dating system, the structure of universities and hospitals. It matters because you cannot read Western history, politics, or culture without it.

## How it started

- **Jesus (~4 BCE-33 CE)** was born in Roman Palestine, raised in Nazareth, and began preaching around age 30. He taught in parables, claimed authority to forgive sins, attracted crowds, threatened both Jewish religious authorities and Roman occupiers, and was crucified in Jerusalem. His followers said he rose from the dead three days later.
- The movement was originally a Jewish reform movement. The disciples kept Jewish law and worshipped in the Temple.
- **Paul of Tarsus (~5-65 CE)** changed everything. A Pharisee who initially persecuted Christians, he converted after a vision on the road to Damascus, then spent three decades planting churches across the Roman Empire and writing letters that became most of the New Testament. He argued that gentiles (non-Jews) could become Christian without converting to Judaism first. That unlocked global spread.
- For ~300 years Christians were a persecuted minority. Roman emperors used them as scapegoats, fed them to lions, burned them in Nero's gardens.
- **Constantine's conversion (~312 CE)** flipped the script. Before the Battle of Milvian Bridge he claimed a vision of a cross with the words "in this sign conquer." He won, legalized Christianity with the Edict of Milan (313 CE), and convened the Council of Nicaea (325 CE) which fixed core doctrine.
- **380 CE**: Emperor Theodosius made Christianity the state religion of Rome. In less than 70 years it went from illegal to mandatory.
- **The Great Schism (1054 CE)** split Christianity into the Catholic Church (Rome, Latin, pope) and the Eastern Orthodox Church (Constantinople, Greek, patriarchs). The break was over authority, theology, and a single Latin word added to the creed.
- **The Reformation (1517 CE)**: a German monk named **Martin Luther** nailed 95 theses to a church door in Wittenberg protesting the sale of indulgences. The printing press spread his arguments fast. Catholicism fractured into Protestant denominations and Europe went to war over religion for the next 130 years.

## Core beliefs

- **Monotheism with a twist.** One God, but three persons: Father, Son (Jesus), and Holy Spirit. This is the Trinity, fixed at Nicaea. Other Abrahamic religions reject it as compromising monotheism.
- **Jesus is God incarnate.** Fully human and fully divine. He died for the sins of humanity and rose again.
- **Salvation by faith.** Believers are saved through faith in Jesus, not by good works alone. Protestants and Catholics differ sharply on the details. Catholics emphasize faith plus sacraments plus works; Protestants emphasize faith alone (*sola fide*).
- **Second coming.** Jesus will return, judge the living and the dead, and establish God's kingdom.
- **Universal mission.** Christians are commanded to spread the faith. This is why Christianity, like Islam, became a global religion while Judaism and Hinduism largely did not.

## Sacred texts

- **The Bible** has two parts. The Old Testament is essentially the Hebrew Bible inherited from Judaism (Genesis through Malachi). The New Testament is the Christian addition: four gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John) telling Jesus's life, the Acts of the Apostles, Paul's letters, and Revelation.
- The New Testament was written in Greek between ~50-100 CE. The canon was fixed in the late 4th century.
- Catholics and Orthodox include additional books (the Apocrypha) that Protestants reject.

## Major branches

- **Catholic (~50% of Christians, ~1.3 billion).** Largest branch. Centered in Rome, led by the pope, organized into a global hierarchy. Dominant in Latin America, Southern Europe, the Philippines, parts of Africa.
- **Protestant (~37%, ~900 million).** Split off from Catholicism in the 1500s. Not one church but thousands of denominations: Lutheran, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Presbyterian, Pentecostal, Evangelical. Dominant in Northern Europe, the US, sub-Saharan Africa, South Korea.
- **Eastern Orthodox (~12%, ~280 million).** Separate since 1054. Dominant in Russia, Greece, the Balkans, Ukraine. More liturgical, more mystical, more icon-heavy.
- **Smaller groups**: Coptic (Egypt), Ethiopian Orthodox, Mormons, Jehovah's Witnesses, plus thousands of independent African churches.

## Demographics and geography

- Dominant religion in the Americas (~78% Christian), Europe (~75%), and sub-Saharan Africa (~62%).
- **United States**: ~63% Christian (down from ~90% in 1972). Roughly 40% Protestant, 20% Catholic, plus smaller groups.
- **India**: ~2.3% Christian (~30 million), concentrated in Kerala, Goa, and the Northeast.
- Fastest growth today is in sub-Saharan Africa and parts of Asia. Christianity is shrinking in Europe and the US, growing fastest in Nigeria, China, and Brazil.

## Practices

- Sunday worship in church, usually with singing, scripture readings, and a sermon.
- **Baptism**: ritual initiation, with water, as an infant (Catholic, Orthodox, mainline Protestant) or adult (Baptist, Evangelical).
- **Communion (Eucharist)**: bread and wine representing Jesus's body and blood. Catholics believe the bread literally becomes the body of Christ (transubstantiation). Protestants generally treat it as symbolic.
- Prayer, often the Lord's Prayer taught by Jesus.
- Christmas (December 25, birth of Jesus) and Easter (resurrection, the bigger of the two theologically).

## Modern controversies

- **The decline of religion in the West.** The fastest-growing religious category in the US and Europe is "none." Among Americans under 30, ~40% report no religious affiliation.
- **Sexual abuse scandals.** The Catholic Church has paid out billions in settlements globally. The 2002 Boston Globe investigation cracked it open in the US; similar scandals have surfaced in Ireland, Australia, Germany, Chile.
- **Megachurches and the prosperity gospel.** Pastors like Joel Osteen and Kenneth Copeland preach that God wants believers rich and healthy. Mainstream theologians from across denominations call it a betrayal of Jesus's actual teachings.
- **LGBTQ inclusion.** Mainline Protestant denominations (Episcopal, Lutheran, Methodist) have moved toward acceptance, often splitting in the process. Catholics, Orthodox, and Evangelicals largely have not.
- **Women's ordination.** Catholics and Orthodox do not ordain women. Most Protestants do.
- **Evangelical politics in the US.** White evangelicals voted ~80% for Donald Trump in 2016, 2020, and 2024. The fusion of Christianity with American nationalism (Christian nationalism) is one of the most consequential religious shifts of this century.
- **Persecution.** Open Doors estimates ~360 million Christians face high persecution, mostly in North Korea, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Nigeria, and parts of the Middle East.

## What people get wrong

- "All Christians believe the same thing." The gap between a Quaker, a Greek Orthodox priest, a Texas megachurch pastor, and a Vatican cardinal is enormous. Calling them one group is like calling all democracies one government.
- Christianity didn't replace paganism overnight. Many Christmas, Easter, and saint traditions were grafted onto older pagan festivals to ease conversion.
- Jesus didn't found a religion called Christianity. He was a Jew teaching Jews. The religion was organized after his death by people like Paul.
- The crusades are real history but a small fraction of Christian history. Most of what Christianity built were universities, hospitals, charities, and the legal concept that humans have inherent worth.

## What to carry away

- One Jewish reform movement in a Roman backwater conquered the empire that crucified its founder. That is the unlikely story.
- The Bible's Old Testament is Judaism's Hebrew Bible. The New Testament is the Christian add-on.
- Three big branches: Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant. The Reformation in 1517 created the third.
- In the West today, Christianity is contracting in influence but still shapes everything you walk through. In the global South, it is the fastest-growing major religion.
- In one sentence: Christianity is the most successful religious movement in history, built on the claim that an executed Jewish carpenter was God.
