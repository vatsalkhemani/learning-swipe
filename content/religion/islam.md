# Islam: the youngest of the Abrahamic religions and the fastest growing

## What is Islam and why it matters

Islam is the world's second-largest religion, with about 1.9 billion followers, roughly 24% of humanity, and the fastest-growing major religion by birth rate. It traces its origin to **Muhammad (570-632 CE)**, a merchant in Mecca who said he began receiving revelations from God through the angel Gabriel in 610 CE. Those revelations became the Quran. Within Muhammad's lifetime most of the Arabian Peninsula converted. Within 100 years of his death, Muslim armies and traders had built an empire from Spain to India, preserving Greek philosophy, advancing mathematics and medicine, and laying the groundwork for what Europeans later called the Renaissance. It matters today because Islam shapes the lives of 1.9 billion people across 50+ countries, sits at the center of global geopolitics, and is the most misunderstood major religion in the West.

## How it started

- **Muhammad** was born in Mecca, a trading hub and pilgrimage center for Arab polytheism. Orphaned young, raised by his uncle, became a respected merchant.
- At 40 he began retreating to a cave outside Mecca to meditate. In 610 CE, he said the angel **Gabriel** appeared and told him to recite. The words he recited became the first verses of the Quran.
- For 13 years he preached in Mecca, calling people to abandon polytheism and worship one God. He won a small group of followers and was violently opposed by Mecca's pagan elite, whose religious economy he was threatening.
- **The Hijra (622 CE)**: Muhammad and his followers fled persecution in Mecca and migrated to the nearby city of Yathrib (later renamed Medina). This migration is year 1 of the Islamic calendar. The community went from persecuted minority to governing majority.
- For the next decade, Muhammad was both religious leader and political ruler of Medina. He fought wars with Mecca, eventually conquered it in 630 CE, and destroyed the pagan idols at the Kaaba (the cube-shaped building Muslims now circle during the hajj).
- He died in 632 CE. Most of Arabia was Muslim. There was no clear succession plan.
- **The Sunni-Shia split (656 CE)** came from that succession question. One faction said the community should elect leaders (caliphs); they became Sunnis. Another said leadership belonged to Muhammad's bloodline through his cousin and son-in-law **Ali**; they became Shias. Ali was eventually assassinated, his son Husayn was killed at the Battle of Karbala (680 CE), and Shia Islam built its identity around that martyrdom.
- The Rashidun Caliphate (632-661), then the Umayyad Caliphate (661-750), then the Abbasid Caliphate (750-1258) spread Islam from the Atlantic coast of Morocco to the Indus River. The Abbasid period, centered in Baghdad, was the golden age of Islamic science and philosophy.

## Core beliefs

- **Strict monotheism (tawhid).** There is no god but God (Allah, the Arabic word for God, used by Arab Christians too). The Trinity is rejected.
- **Muhammad is the final prophet.** Islam recognizes a chain of prophets including Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. Jesus is honored as a prophet and even as the Messiah, but not as God's son. Muhammad is the "seal of the prophets," the last.
- **The Quran is the literal word of God.** Not Muhammad's words about God, but God's own words delivered through Muhammad. This claim about the text is much stronger than Christian or Jewish claims about their scriptures.
- **Predestination.** God knows and decrees all things. This is more emphasized in Islamic theology than in mainstream Christian theology.
- **Day of Judgment.** Bodies are resurrected, deeds are weighed, paradise or hell follows.

## The Five Pillars

These are the obligations every Muslim must observe.

- **Shahada**: declaring "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is his messenger." Saying this with belief makes a person Muslim.
- **Salat**: five daily prayers facing Mecca, at dawn, noon, afternoon, sunset, and night.
- **Zakat**: charity, roughly 2.5% of accumulated wealth per year, given to the poor.
- **Sawm**: fasting from food, drink, and sex during daylight hours throughout the lunar month of Ramadan.
- **Hajj**: pilgrimage to Mecca, required once in a lifetime for those who can afford it. About 2-3 million Muslims make hajj every year, the largest annual gathering on Earth.

## Sacred texts

- **The Quran**: 114 chapters (suras), arranged roughly longest to shortest rather than chronologically. Compiled into a single authoritative version around 650 CE under the third caliph, **Uthman**. Memorized in full by millions of Muslims (called hafiz).
- **The Hadith**: collected sayings and actions of Muhammad, compiled in the 9th century. Sunnis and Shias use different collections. Hadith fill in what the Quran doesn't cover and form the basis of Islamic law (sharia).
- **Sharia**: not a single book but a system of legal rulings derived from Quran and hadith, with significant variation across schools.

## Major branches

- **Sunni (~85-90% of Muslims).** The mainstream. Includes most Muslims in the Arab world, Turkey, South Asia, Indonesia, and Africa. Within Sunni Islam, four schools of jurisprudence (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) handle legal interpretation.
- **Shia (~10-15%).** Dominant in Iran (~90%), Iraq (~65%), Bahrain, Azerbaijan. Significant minorities in Lebanon, Yemen, Saudi Arabia's eastern province. Believe religious authority passed through Ali and his descendants (the imams). The largest Shia subgroup, the Twelvers, believe the 12th imam went into hiding in 874 CE and will return as the Mahdi.
- **Sufi**: not a separate branch but a mystical tradition running through both Sunni and Shia Islam. Emphasizes direct experience of God through poetry, music, dance (the whirling dervishes), and devotion to spiritual teachers. **Rumi** (1207-1273), the bestselling poet in America for decades, was a Sufi.

## Demographics and geography

- **Indonesia** is the largest Muslim country, ~230 million Muslims (~87% of the population). Most people are surprised by this; Islam's center of gravity is not Arab.
- **Pakistan** (~225 million), **India** (~200 million), **Bangladesh** (~150 million), **Nigeria**, **Egypt**, **Iran**, **Turkey** follow.
- South Asia (India + Pakistan + Bangladesh) holds more Muslims than the entire Arab world.
- **India**: ~14% Muslim (~200 million), making it the country with the third-largest Muslim population on Earth.
- **United States**: ~1.1% Muslim (~3.5 million), growing fast through immigration and conversion.
- Islam will likely surpass Christianity as the world's largest religion by ~2070 if current trends hold, mostly driven by higher birth rates.

## Practices and life cycle

- Mosque attendance, especially the **Friday congregational prayer (Jumu'ah)**.
- **Halal dietary rules**: no pork, no alcohol, meat must be slaughtered ritually. Similar in structure to Jewish kosher.
- Daily prayer is structured: ritual washing (wudu), facing Mecca, set sequence of words and bodily postures.
- Boys are circumcised, often as infants.
- Marriage is highly valued; celibacy is generally not honored in Islam the way it is in Catholicism.
- Funerals are simple, burial happens fast (usually within 24 hours), no embalming, no caskets in most traditions.

## Modern controversies

- **Terrorism and the legacy of ISIS, Al-Qaeda, Taliban.** A tiny fraction of Muslims globally, but world-shaping in impact. The post-9/11 era has reshaped airport security, immigration policy, foreign wars, and Muslim communities everywhere.
- **Women's rights debates.** Hijab, niqab, education, driving (Saudi women won the right to drive only in 2018), male guardianship laws, female genital cutting (more cultural than religious but persists in some Muslim regions). Practice varies enormously: Indonesia and Turkey produced female heads of state; Afghanistan under the Taliban bars girls from secondary school.
- **Apostasy and blasphemy laws.** Leaving Islam is technically punishable by death in classical Islamic law and is still capital in several countries. Salman Rushdie has been hunted since 1989 for *The Satanic Verses*.
- **Sunni-Shia geopolitics.** Saudi Arabia (Sunni) and Iran (Shia) have fought proxy wars across Yemen, Syria, Iraq, Lebanon for decades.
- **Integration in Europe.** Muslim immigrant communities in France, Germany, the UK have produced both successful integration and sharp tensions: hijab bans, mosque controversies, terrorism, the question of whether European secularism can accommodate visible religion.
- **Islamophobia in the West**, especially post-9/11. Hate crimes, surveillance programs, immigration bans (the 2017 Trump "Muslim ban").

## What people get wrong

- **Jihad** primarily means struggle, including inner spiritual struggle to be a better person. Military jihad is one form with strict conditions in classical Islamic law. Translating it as "holy war" loses most of the meaning.
- Most Muslims are not Arab. Indonesians and South Asians together outnumber all Arab Muslims.
- The Quran is not arranged chronologically. The early Meccan verses are gentler; the later Medinan verses, when Muhammad was a head of state, are more legal and at times martial. Cherry-picking either side misrepresents the whole.
- Muslims revere Jesus and his mother Mary; they just don't accept the divinity claim.

## What to carry away

- One merchant in 7th-century Arabia, one set of revelations, two big branches, 1.9 billion followers.
- The split between Sunni and Shia was about who should lead after Muhammad died. It is older than every Christian denomination split.
- The Five Pillars are the practical core: declare, pray, give, fast, pilgrimage.
- Indonesia, not Saudi Arabia, has the most Muslims. India has more Muslims than every Arab country combined except possibly two.
- In one sentence: Islam is a strict monotheism founded in 7th-century Arabia by a merchant who said God spoke through him, now the fastest-growing major religion on Earth.
